## Simplifier VS Optimizer

I have recently learnt that people normally fall into one of two categories.

“Some people are what I call simplifiers and some are optimizers. A simplifier will prefer the easy way to accomplish a task while knowing that some amount of extra effort might have produced a better outcome.

An optimizer looks for the very best solution even if the extra complexity increases the odds of unexpected problems.”

- Scott Adams author of [How to Fail at Almost Everything and Still Win Big: Kind of the Story of My Life](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/17859574-how-to-fail-at-almost-everything-and-still-win-big)

BONUS: You can read this section of the [book here](https://boingboing.net/2013/12/02/simplifiers-and-optimizers-by.html), if interested.

After further research, I found out that it depends on local (or per small unit) and global (or per large unit aka long-term) scale.

The ideal scale is to be a simplifier on the **local scale**, meaning you can focus on one task at a time very well and an optimizer on the **global scale**, meaning in the long-term you can see how optimizing multiple tasks can allow you to reach your goals faster.

I used to be an Optimizer both locally and globally, trying always to do multiple things at once making me more exhausted, experiencing burnout or delayed goals.

## To-Do Lists

Do you write to-do lists?

My intent by the end of this section is to convince you to use them if you do not already. If you do use them already then I hope for you to learn something you didn’t know.

“How often should I write to-do lists?”

That really depends on you, everyone is going to have a slightly different approach.

I often hear you should do day, week, and high-level goals for 3, 6, and 12 months.

I do daily, weekdays, and weekends then goals for 1, 3, 6 and 12 months.

Highly suggest you make your to-do list the night before, it normally takes me 15 mins, but when I was just starting it took me about 30 mins.

**My Tips for to-do lists:**

- You’re the Master** - Remember you’re the master, not the to-do list! Meaning if you don’t follow the to-do list exactly because something came up **be kind to yourself**!
- One thing at a time** - Give yourself permission to focus on one thing at a time, it’s normal for priorities to change and unforeseen circumstances to throw you off track. You have the choice to adjust and reprioritize
- Priory tasks** - If you asked yourself if I only do this one task today, will I be satisfied today? And what if you don't do it?
  - Give yourself at **most **3 primary tasks daily
  - Do it first!
  - These should be ideally in your deep work time slots
- Buffer time** - Give yourself more time than you estimate, it will almost always take longer and you will feel good
- Too complex** - If the task is complex, break it down into smaller tasks
- Get it off your mind** - If you have anxiety about a task, then you haven’t broken it down or planned it enough.
- Know yourself **- Know when you are most productive and plan your deep work then

Your morning routine is the **most powerful habit** you can do, when you start your day off on the right foot, then it sets up how you will feel, act and do for the rest of the day.

Tips from many books and resources:

- Keep your phone out of reach of the bed**
- Keep your notifications off during deep work
- Don’t snooze, get straight out of bed
- Read your visions, goals and values
- Repeat some affirmations
- Make your bed
- Tackle the number one priority on your to-do list before you look at your phone or email

If you feel like a to-do list is not working for you, it is often easier to write a “not-to-do list”. Where you write the things you want to avoid, or stop and stick to it!

**TIP: **If you find yourself skipping the important things, book them in your calendar. Book when you are going to the gym, spending time with loved ones, and schedule your bedtime.

## Batching Tasks

Just like batching inference in data science, batching tasks will save you a lot of time!

I am going to explain with an example.

**Coffee example**

How many coffees/teas do you require or make a day?

How long does each take to make? Maybe roughly 5 -10 mins right? So if you made 3 of them:

~ 15 - 30 minutes per day
~ 105 - 210 minutes per week
~ 5460 - 10,920 minutes (or 91 - 182 hours) per year

~ 91 hours a year to make coffee!???? That's nuts!!!

Ok so if you made the 3 all at once, then pre-portioned it? Ok, so it takes maybe an extra minute? So it takes:

~ 6 - 11 minutes per day
~ 42 -  77 minutes per week
~ 2184 - 4004 minutes (or 36.4 - 67 hours) per year

Congratulations, you saved ~ 60 hours a year

I am not saying to batch everything especially if it makes you happy. But I hate coffee, I only drink it for the reaction, so I make instant coffee with very little boiled water and add milk so it is cold and I can chug it as fast as I can. So if you are someone who loves coffee and it makes you happy, then don’t do this technique! But just think of this example of things in life that you repeat and do multiple times a day.

**Other tasks to potentially batch:**
- Laundry - the longest you can go until you need to do a wash (I can go about 4-5 weeks)
- Errands - (I maybe do once a month, I order stuff in)
- Cooking - meal prep
- Checking your phone, slack or emails
- etc

### Meal Prep

Meal prep means something different for each person.

I used to think meal prep was crazy as it loses the fun of variety and it takes much longer to prepare a plan for a meal but then I switched to it and it actually saves me soooo much time in meal prep.

Everyone is different and the amount of food people require is also different. I have a really high metabolism and my body requires a lot of calories (a.k.a energy) to keep up with what I do. Thus, for many years of my life, I required 9 meals a day, recently I have reduced it to 6 with more snacks, but if I spent an hour for each meal I would lose 6 - 9 hours each day, so I have to meal prep! So whenever I make a meal, I make at least 3 meals.

And recently I have got to the point where I meal prep 1.5 month's worth of dinners

I have made the same breakfast for myself for almost 2 years straight, I don’t need to think about what I should make for breakfast, the habits are grained in my head and it makes me happy.

# **Pomodoro Timer**

Everyone struggles with focus and motivation at some point, especially when there’s a lot of tasks to handle. This leads to mental fatigue, anxiety, and burnout.
A great time management tool we can use to help us beat procrastination, multitasking, and other bad productivity tendencies, is the ***Pomodoro timer. ***

This is a technique invented in the 1980s by Francesco Cirillo that trains your mind to focus better on tasks by limiting the amount of time you stay focused, and ensuring you take breaks to restore the energy used during the tasks.

How does this work?

You start your timer and after every **25 **minutes, you take a **5** minutes break.
If you can focus for longer hours, then the technique suggests that after every **4 **hours, take a **15 to 30** minutes break.

25 minutes of work —> 5 minutes break —> 25 minutes of work
4 hours of work —> 15-30 minutes break —> 4 hours of work

## **Benefits of using a pomodoro timer**

- It helps you plan your time efficiently**
Sometimes we tend to underestimate or overestimate how long it takes for us to complete a particular task. The Pomodoro technique helps us to divide work into planned chunks such that we have a real sense of how much work we can do within a certain timeframe.

- It helps us overcome procrastination**
A task feels more manageable when done in a single-timed session. Instead of putting several hours of work at a go, you will only need to stay focused for 25 minutes which is enough to finish the task without losing focus. This helps you to get more work done and reduce the anxiety that comes with procrastination.

- It reduces distractions**
Often, we find ourselves doing things which take up the hours we should have been doing meaningful things. The pomodoro timer helps you to remain focused on doing a particular task without having to scroll through social media or do other tasks that might take up your concentration and waste your time.

- It’s simple and flexible**
The method is easy to use and can be used even without special training. You only need to turn on your timer while starting a task and after 25 minutes, you take a break, and repeat.
