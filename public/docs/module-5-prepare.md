## Using The Job Application As A Cheat Sheet:

Studying the job description helps you understand what the employer is looking for.
- Take the job description, and read the key qualifications and job requirements.
  - Watch for the adjectives they use, if you can mimic them then great!
- Copy them into a document
- Prepare an answer for each bullet point (write, read, say, practice)
- Then try to incorporate those key points into your answer indirectly (it’s ok sometimes to do it directly)
- Then for the qualifications and requirements add some of them to your “tell me about yourself” or “why did you apply” questions.

## The S.T.A.R Approach

If you have never heard of this approach or would like a quick reminder [this article](https://resume.io/blog/star-method?ga_utm_source=google&ga_utm_medium=ppc&ga_utm_campaign=1592605423&ga_utm_term=&gclid=Cj0KCQjw8_qRBhCXARIsAE2AtRZJNwuhzfBsQA_qrYSk5q31d1t0mAE_hTj_utUhmHZELhrvjcuVCx0aAiTpEALw_wcB) is pretty good as a refresher, I will provide some examples related more to data science.

You want to tailor every open-ended interview question like this. It is often used for behavioural interview questions but it is also really good at explaining experiences and technical projects.

### S.T.A.R.

- Situation **- Share the nature of the event, project or challenge.
- Task **- Describe your specific responsibility in the process.
- Action** - Steps were undertaken to move towards an outcome
- Result** - End results of your individual involvement.

### *New Alternative* Option -  C.A.R.

- Challenge**
- Action**
- Result**

### Why CAR is Better than STAR

The bigger the challenge or emotions you bring out of the story the more the result sounds impressive

It’s a story remember let it flow with a beginning, middle and end. Recall the Pixar story pitch method in “Sell Yourself as a Product”

Once upon a time ____.
Every day, ____.
One day ____.
Because of that, ______.
Because of that, ____.
Until Finally _____

### When to use the STAR (or CAR) method

- Tell me about a time when….
- Have you ever….
- Give me an example of….
- Describe when you….
- What do you do when…

## How To Answer: Tell Me About Yourself

Another way to ask this question is “Walk me through your resume”

This is where our exercises from selling yourself as a product should come into play.

When you give your summary, the goal is to use the words they used in the job posting.

Use the selling yourself as a product technique - [“How to sell yourself”](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1WZNA-I7K51fzCnKhllReiipOQhl6uJoJ841wdMpcFMQ/edit?usp=sharing).

You can also tailor your response using the S.T.A.R. method and the Pixar story pitch

**Rough structure **(This is not the only outline, it is just what I use)

- Background **(Very quickly)
- Experience**
- Path**
- What you are doing/learning **now **(and how it helps them indirectly)
- What you are working **towards** (and make it what you can do for them or why you are a great fit for the role) or a little bit about you that makes you** unique **(The more you stand out the more you will be remembered)

**NOTE**: Each point should still have value (ideally quantitative values) and try to cut the fluff

The more experience you have the more you can just talk about your experience, if you have little or no experience, it’s ok to talk about your passions.

**Example:**

Ya sure, so I am a data scientist with a background in software engineering and statistics, specialized in databases and AI.**[BACKGROUND]** I have 2+ of industry experience, the 1.5 years of industry experience was focused more on the complete data science project lifecycle: Accessing AI readiness and data quality, Project Scoping and Proposing, Client and Project Managing, Extracting, Transforming and Loading (ETL), Data Wrangling, Research and Developing (R&D) Various Machine Learning Models, then Containerizing or Deploying for Clients, for time series problems. **[EXPERIENCE]**

Since NRCan’s projects were short-term consultant projects where I never really got to see my hard work in production, for the last year I have focused on MLOps and ML in production **[PATH]**, where I am currently doing more management and tech lead work on a small team of 4 data scientists, 3 data engineers and a project manager while implementing the MLOps standards in our department, I would like to get back to dealing more with models **[NOW]**. And as I have so much to learn and many career goals, I am looking for a position where I can soak up as much knowledge and skills from a senior $JOB_TITLE while continuing to use my mentoring skills to teach and grow the team knowledge **[TOWARDS/UNIQUE], **thus this is why I know $COMPANY_NAME is an excellent match. So ya, that is a little bit about me, let me know if you have any questions you want me to dive into more details.

**NOTE: **This example is on the longer side, but I do talk quickly, after trying to slow it down it only takes me about 1.5 minutes to say aloud, where the goal is to keep it around 2 minutes.

## How To Answer: Why Are You Interested In Us

I used to stress about this a lot early in my career. Although it is very important, as it shows interest and passion for the company, the more you actually know about the company/team/products is great.

On that note, I only prepared for this question (if it’s not my dream job, aka on my list of 10-15 companies) within 10 mins prior to the interview. The reason is, that I personally don’t care about any employer, and until I have the contract in front of me, so I am not going to buy in emotionally. I suppose you could say I have trust issues with employers lol (more on this in the next module) so I follow a “don’t talk about, be about it” methodology where if it’s in the offer in writing then I don’t believe what they are pitching.

### Variations:

- “Why are you interested in us?”
- “Why did you apply?”
- “What interested you in this position?”
- “Why are you interested in this company?”
- “What Can You Bring to the Company?”
- “Why should we hire you?” (This one is pretty much the same but we just need to add a little more about what makes you stand out compared to other candidates)

### How to prepare for these questions: (~ 5 mins)

- Look at the job description (as a cheat sheet)
- Match your requirements
- Highlight your skills
- Record the adjectives they use when describing particular skills and try to incorporate them into your explanation (called *mirroring *in psychology)
- What do you like about the company?
  - Business model
  - Values
  - Mission
  - Business goal
  - The impact
  - Types of problems they solve
  - The tech stack
  - Culture/environment
  - The type of work
  - The people the help

- Tell a story about your journey to applying, or reaching out
- Do share your long-term interest in the company
- Do share what products you use of the company’s
- Do reference someone you know who works there
- Do reference someone who showed you the position
- Do mention what you are looking for in a company

## How To Answer: Experience Or Project

This is a very important part of all interviews!

### Variations

“Could you walk me through a project you have done?”
“Could you tell me about your experience?”

### Tackling It

We want to follow the S.T.A.R. approach.

We want to go into detail about the problem and then give them a chance to ask questions about the tech stack and to dig into it more, and trust me the smaller the company or the more technical the interviewer is, the more they will dig into your explanation.

This is why we have been keeping track of our challenges and struggles in module 1, so we can express them and talk about how we overcame them.

### Example - data science project experience

**Situation:** I was working for a retail company and we noticed that our online sales had decreased over the past few months. We wanted to identify the root cause of the problem and find ways to increase our sales.

**Task: **As a data analyst, my task was to analyze the data related to online sales and identify any patterns or trends that could help us understand why our sales had decreased. I worked with the e-commerce team to gather data from our website, marketing campaigns, and sales data.

**Action: **After collecting the data, I conducted a thorough analysis and found that our website's load time had increased, causing customers to abandon their carts before completing their purchases. Based on this analysis, I recommended that we invest in improving our website's load time.

**Result: **After implementing these recommendations, our online sales increased by 25% within the next quarter. Additionally, we saw an increase in customer satisfaction ratings for our website's speed and user experience. Our team was thrilled with the results, and the company's leadership recognized my contribution to this success.

# Asking Questions at the end of the interview:

You should **always **have questions!
“The questions you ask, tell people more about you compared to anything else” - Mark Cuban
This is especially true for the interviews
Don’t ask questions you can find on the internet, only ask questions that the interviewer can answer for you and showcase your research, interest, skills and experience.
This will help you stand out!
### How to ask better questions:

1. Produce your questions
2. Improve your questions
3. Prioritize your questions (a.k.a rank them from best to worst)

### Important Questions

In this section, we will talk about important questions that are a must.

### Question #1:

If they do not tell you the interview process make sure to ask.

“Do you mind telling me the interview process (or stages) for this position?”

This is important because you want to schedule them in similar time positioning to have the offers to us at roughly the same time, to give us the most leverage possible to use the companies to compete against themselves. (a.k.a contract negotiation)

### Question #2

“As I am very interested in this position I was wondering if there is anything you are unsure about my experience or profile that is preventing you from moving me forward?”

It is very direct so try to say it the best you can. It allows you to handle objections or any concerns and show why those weaknesses are actually strengths (sound familiar? Yes, module 3, selling yourself as a product worksheet). This question also allows you to get an idea if you should keep your hopes up or “Thank you, next” (*in Ariana Grande voice)*.

If you get a “Well, we still are interviewing all candidates and we’ll get back to you”That is a polite way of, you didn’t knock their socks off (a.k.a. “Thank you, next” in *Ariana Grande's voice)*

Because if they loved you, they wouldn’t risk losing you and would let you know you will be moving to the next round.

But this is actually **great **because now we can reflect on the interview, and look at things to improve when it’s still fresh in our minds.

Then if they do get back to you, great I had 0 expectations what a great day!

However, this is how I think, *they couldn’t find anything better, so they circled back to me*… ah I see, I will remember this when I accept your offer, delay the start date and look for better opportunities 🙃

### Question #2 - Improved:

“Would it be okay if I recapped what is that I think you’re looking for in this next hire?”

—- We’ve never had a no —---

—-- State recap—-

Did I miss anything?

—- They answer, most likely no because we are great listeners 🙂 —

“Thank you again for taking the time to meet with me today. I enjoyed getting to learn more about the position and I’m very excited about moving forward. I’d like to quickly ask,

Was there anything about my background that concerns you about my ability to do this job? I’d love the opportunity to clarify or confirm any of your concerns.”

Now they will state if they have concerns.

And you can handle their concern with this framework:

- Agree
- Clarify
- Diffuse
- Discuss

If the concern is true => admit to it and then flip it to a positive

If the concern is not true => “Sorry that is my fault, I should have done a better job clarifying that” then explain it

# How To Research Company Before Interview

Candidates who perform the best in interviews do not hastily scroll through the company’s website. They take time to research things like:
- Industry trends
- The company’s products ( some even sign up to use them)
- Customers
- Competitors
- Latest company news
- Company’s history
- Different teams, departments, and leaders

This type of research helps you to ask better questions, and get more confident.

**Note:** You can use tools such as PerplexityAI to help you do the research and save time. You just need to prompt it like ChatGPT

For example, *“I’m interviewing for a data science job at Bell, please tell me the company’s performance history for the last 3 years,  what products they have, their current leadership team, and any latest news.”*
