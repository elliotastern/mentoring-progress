## Why Do We Need A Networking Script?

Our goal is to minimize our time creating & typing messages that we have before.

You don’t need to focus your energy on typing the message. You can do this when waiting in line, or during any downtime.

Another reason is so you can track conversations and find a solution that works for you.

## METHOD: Building A Relationship Via DMs (Delayed Initial Ask)

### Connection Request Message

**Goal**: Have the prospect click “accept.”
This is the message attached to the connection request on Linkedin. The question we are looking to answer is: “Why is this person trying to connect with me?”

Tips:
- Keep it short
- Less is more
- Personalize the message
- Use their first name
- Be respectful and genuine
- Find commonality whenever possible
  - Alumni
  - Similar interests
  - Connections in common

### 1st Follow-up Message

**Goal**: Build trust & rapport, start a conversation
This is the first message they receive after the connection request is accepted on LinkedIn. Cater to their goals, interests and passions. Make it about them.

Tips:
- Share some gratitude
- Theory of Reciprocity
  - Offer up your network
- Be friendly & conversational
- Ask a question

### 2nd Follow-up Message

**Goal**: Create familiarity, stay top of mind, provide value
This is the message they receive if they DO NOT respond to the prior message on LinkedIn. Give them insight into who you are, what you do, and how you can add value

Tips:
- Ask questions to determine pain points
- Provide insight about what you do and how you've helped people similar to them
- Soft Call-To-Action (CTA)

### 3rd Follow-up Message

**Goal**: Schedule a Meeting, and move them off of LinkedIn
This is the message they receive if they DO NOT respond to the prior message.
Confidence is important here, be direct without coming across as too pushy or salesy.

Tips:
- Gentle reminder / follow up on prior message
  - Remind them you’ve been connected a few days now
- Keep it short
  - Keep in mind their perspective, “What’s in it for me”
- Speak to what problems/challenges they are facing
  - Share information about a solution
- Call to Action: Suggest a meeting
  - Put the ball in their court
  - Offer up a link to your calendar

# The Exit Clause For Cold Emailing And Cold Messaging

If you want to triple your cold email response rate, always use the  “Exit Clause.”

Most people have a common way of closing cold emails and messages. They use statements such as:

“Looking forward to your reply.”

“Let’s schedule a call, here’s my Calendly:”

“Thanks in advance.” Etc.

What similarities do you see in these statements? And why don’t I recommend using such?
These statements have the expectation of the next step as if it’s already certain. That puts a lot of pressure on the receiver and it can make them feel uncomfortable.

Often, this will lead them to ignore your email or worse delete it.

What’s the best way to close your emails and messages then?

For example:

**“Lastly, I know your time is valuable and I know this is a big ask coming from a stranger. If it’s too much right now, that’s totally fine! Either way, I hope you’re staying safe and healthy.” **

This “Exit Clause” is more likely to get a response because it takes pressure from the recipient.

It shows that you are treating them like human beings, you are recognizing their time, and you not only care about yourself but you are also making a note about them.

That’s the right note to start your relationship.

# How To Message A Recruiter

## **By Teron Foster**

❌"Hi, Teron,
I graduated from … University in Aug 2021. I applied for student jobs at VMware. Would you please accept my connection request and review my applications?"
Above, is an example of the many messages that I receive on a daily basis. I appreciate the effort of reaching out, however; please provide more context to your InMail messages when reaching out to recruiters, the above message does not provide any value or does it separate you from the rest of the candidates that are reaching out as well.”
Honestly, recruiters are** busy** and the above message requires the recruiter to do **extra work**. Please **be direct** and avoid sending your thesis statement. LOL
Try this template when reaching out to recruiters:
✅"Hi (Recruiter),
My name is (Name) and I’m a (Title). When you have a second, I would love the chance to hop on a quick call to introduce myself and get to know you and your career trajectory. I'm very intrigued to understand how you worked to where you are in your career today and VMware seems like a great company, can we chat about your experience so far there as well?
Also, I would like to discuss how my (Specific Skill) and experiences (highlight a couple of relatable experiences that can relate to the role you’re applying for) might match the (Job Title/Req #) that I’ve applied for (If You Already Applied). Thanks for connecting and I hope you have a great day!"
