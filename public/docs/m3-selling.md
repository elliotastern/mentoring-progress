# What Is Selling Yourself As A Product

Now that you have designed your product, you are a salesperson, go sell it! You have to know how to sell your product, or else it won’t be beneficial.

Optimize yourself for the market. Show your buyers who you are, what your strengths are, and what you will add to their table.

The summary you created in the [worksheet](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qmNnXU_ZOM8CDOzziaGQGv9sWTmHwC3HWjd4VJsdA2w/edit?usp=sharing) should be a place to start when crafting your LinkedIn, resume, cover letter, Github, and even how to answer the “Tell me about yourself” in an interview.

Next, it will help you narrow your scope for networking and applying for what is your ideal employer.

If you can’t narrow the scope in the application process you can narrow it in the interviews by digging into what type of employer they are. It will allow you to target your questions to the employer based on your “must-haves” (written in the exercise).

Then when you are comparing offers, you can reflect on which employers meet more of your “must-haves” and “nice-to-haves”.

Furthermore, it will also prepare you for the objections (concerns) in your interviewers that could arise and should shape your answers to interview questions (more on this topic in module 6).

Lastly, you should remind the employer of what you bring to the table during the contract negotiation stage (more on this topic in module 6).

# The Foundations Of Selling Yourself

## **Attunement**

This is the ability to get out of your head and get into the head of the person you are trying to sell to.

This can be done in 2 ways:
### **Understanding their perspective**

Attunement is not about coercion, it’s about seeing and understanding things through the buyer’s eyes.
Learn from the hirer’s perspective by trying to hire.
Sometimes you just need to put yourself in the shoes of the hiring manager and look at things from their perspective. An idea is, to create a job posting and have interactions with candidates
Pick your top 10 most desired applicants
After picking, try to answer these questions
- Why I’m I picking these people?
- How well do I trust these people to solve my problem?
- What bias am I assuming?
How you rate the best applicant is most probably how the hiring manager would do it. Present yourself in the best ways possible if you want to be considered for the job.
Another idea is to study those people who just landed a job and how they marketed themselves to the employer, or spark a convo and find out more info

Understanding how you make your decision can help you understand how any other hirer would make their decision
Let’s relate this to an interview. If they are looking for XYZ and you have XYZ but decide to showcase ABC instead, will that help you? NO! Sell what they are looking for.

I recently heard a quote in an interview by a National Basketball Association (NBA) player, where the interviewer asked him what was the struggle of staying in the G-League (which is the league below the NBA) when the NBA calls them up to join the NBA.

His response was “*A big reason guys get stuck in the G-League is that they don’t realize the position they’re trying out for. It’s like going to a job interview thinking you’re going to be the CFO of the company, and they’re looking for someone to clean the bathrooms.*”

The point of this quote is, to impress the buyer, you need to know what they are looking for and showcase that. If you are trying to break into the industry with no experience and you want to go straight to only doing Machine Learning, take a step back and remember where you are in your career. Once you get into the company, it is a lot easier to relocate responsibilities.

### **Mirroring strategy**** **

Psychology shows that people are naturally connected to people they share similarities. In this book, they discussed a study by many psychologists and sociologists about how you subtly mimic your buyers:
  - Mannerisms
  - Words they use
  - Their perspectives

The takeaway from this strategy is that the more you are naturally similar to your buyers, the more they are comfortable with you. This will lead them to feel connected and the more they will be subconsciously willing to buy.

This can be done in many subtle ways. It takes a lot of patience to understand how they are thinking and listen to the words others say, and the mannerisms they use.

**Example: Selling a service**

If a potential mentee comes to me and says they are confused and they need a **guide **on how to get their **first job** in the industry.

Then when am explaining my program, I use

“I help you build a **roadmap **to **break into** the industry”

Instead of

“I help you build a **guide **to the land that **first job** in the industry”

They might not be convinced, right? I should use the latter because it is going to stick more. After all, I used the same terminology as they did.

**Example: Interviewing**

Employer: We are looking for someone with a **coding background **(1) who is comfortable with doing a lot of **different data-related tasks** (2) and can **communicate **(3) effectively with **several **(4) teams. Is that something you are **interested in **(5)?

Breakdown of (1):

Bad: Yes, as someone who has a computer science degree, I do have the **software engineering experience**

Good: Yes, as someone who has a computer science degree, I do have that **coding background**

Breakdown of (2):

Bad: …where in my previous role I often had the responsibility to do **various jobs**

Good: …where in my previous role I often had the responsibility to do a lot of **different data-related tasks**

Breakdown of (3 & 4):

Bad: … while often having to **discuss** with **many **teams at once

Good:   … while often having to **communicate **with **several **teams at once

Breakdown of (5)

Bad: … Thus, as my experience and skill align, that is definitely something that **I am willing to do.**

Good: … Thus, as my experience and skill align, that is definitely something that I am **interested in.**

Putting it all together:

Yes, as someone who has a computer science degree, I do have that **coding background **where in my previous role I often had the responsibility to do a lot of **different data-related tasks** while often having to **communicate **with **several **teams at once. Thus as my experience and skills align, that is definitely something that I am **interested in.**

**NOTE: **These techniques do take practice and a lot of listening skills but the good thing is you can practice them in any conversation.
## **Buoyancy**

This is the ability to float in a world of rejection.

**BEFORE**: Positive self-talk is not always the best way to do it. Although it counts, you need to take action. Ask yourself if and how you can do it.

If you figure out how you could solve a problem, convince yourself it’s possible, then act to solve it.

“Can we fix it? yes, we can” - Bob the builder

For example, before an interview, when you are hyping yourself up, instead of saying positive self-talk e.g. “I am going to ace this interview” ask yourself “Why am I going to ace this interview?” You will start answering the right question, and this will truly convince you that you can.

**AFTER**: Acknowledge that objections/rejections are temporary and external, and they don’t impact you at all internally.

After a rejection, ask yourself
- Is this feeling permanent?
- Were you persuasive enough?
- Was the rejection personal? Is their sole purpose to ruin your life?

Think about these questions so that you can have a better next time.

In summary, *Buoyancy* is accepting rejection as part of the process, and we must build a thick skin to stay afloat in the world of rejection. I had a mentor tell me before you that instead of aiming for “Yes,” aim for the “no,” meaning if you acknowledge that every “no” brings us closer to a “Yes,” and we know the stats on how often rejection happens, let’s say 10% and we want 10 interviews then we aim for 90 no’s and take notes why they were no’s (ie 90 failed networking messages). Life is a volume game; the more you do something, the more you will improve.

## **Clarity**

This is the ability to see situations better and help bring to light problems people didn’t even realize they had.

This quote sums this section up, “People don't buy the best product; they buy the fastest product they understand.” Thus, we need to get clarity so we are the fast product they understand

Clarity is proving to the buyer that this product is for them. It is built around not just being a problem solver but a problem finder.

**Problem solver**– someone who simply solves the problem
**Problem finder** – someone who finds out why there is a problem and then solves the problem

In the past, a salesperson had all the information, and the seller would seek the salesperson for information and guidance. Today, the information is everywhere, only one Google search away. Now, they must be experts at curating it.

Become a Curator: 3 Steps
- **Seek **- get the info
- **Sense **- find the meaning
- **Share **- teach someone else

Old salesperson => New salesperson
Assessing info => curating info
Answering questions => asking questions

The following five frames can be useful in providing clarity to those you hope to persuade.

- The Less Frame**
This is based on the selling psychology of when there are fewer than 4 options, the more buyers you will get.

Relating this to the job market goes back to the quote, “The jack of all trades is the master of none, though often, better than a master of one”

Meaning sell to the buyer 3 really good strengths but having 6 strengths in your pocket to switch up to the employer depending on their desire.

- The experience Frame**
This means that when you sell something, customer satisfaction is more about the experience than the materialistic object.

Relating this to the job search, you are not just selling the skills you have but the experience the company and team will benefit from your presence.

**A real mentee example:** there was a mentee who framed their skills and passion for

- The Label Frame**
This is when you give a description or a title that influences others by the association of that word and are more likely to believe it.

I will talk about this frame often in my program. It is about removing the bias of other people to stop undervaluing you.

- “Aspiring Data Scientist” => “Data Scientist”
- “Junior Developer” => “Developer
- “Student Solution architect” => “Data Analyst” (or whatever work you did)
- “Programmer” or “Developer” => “Software Engineer”
- “Undergrad in Computer Science & Statistics” => “Background in software engineer & Statistics”

It is also in the adjectives you use before the nouns. E.g. “little”, “some”, “trying”, “learning”

Examples:
“I know some ML methods including __, ___, and ___”
“I know ML methods including __, ___, and ___”

Do you hear/see the difference?

- The Blemished Frame**
This is when you add a few negative concepts about your product to say it’s not perfect, but it has a lot of value. This shows your honesty.

If you are using this in an interview, don’t just mention the things you don’t have (your blemishes); turn it around to show how you are working on them. Mention that it’s on your to-do list, and with proper training, you’re sure you'll pick it up in no time.

- The Potential Frame**
People find potential more interesting than accomplishments since they will think more deeply about it. So don't fixate on only what I have accomplished but what I want to do, your goals, and your aspirations.

The potential frame is good to use when telling your buyer about your experience or what you want in your career.

You use this to lead the story on your goals and get the employer to imagine the potential impact you will make.

# The Types Of Pitches

### **The One-word Pitch **

This is the use of one word to represent you and your brand. This word has command and forces people to think deeper
  - E.g Data Scientist | Machine Learning Engineer | Limitless

### **The Question Pitch**

Instead of using a statement to phrase your pitch, try using a question. Questions prompt people to come up with their own reasons for agreeing.

Really good for hitting customers' pain points

Examples:
Marking Data Scientist | Tired of not understanding your marketing data? Tired of trying to get more leads but nothing works?

Data Scientist Mentor | Feeling not good enough? Don't think you are fit to be in the data science industry? Feeling lonely, stressed or even depressed? Wondering why you can't break into the industry? Wondering why no one is taking a chance on you? Considering additional education because you don't think you are qualified yet?

### **Rhyme Sales pitch **

A sales pitch that rhymes tend to work better since it is easy to remember. It prompts the buyer to think about it more

### **Subject Line pitch (Often Called “The Hook”)**

Curiosity and uniqueness allow people to click on your email/profile and read more.
A subject line sales pitch can be either obviously useful or mysteriously intriguing.
To be on the advantageous side, draft 2 sales pitches, one using the obviously useful method, and the other using the mysteriously intriguing method. Whichever works in your favor, go for it.
  - Eg. “Day 1 of quitting data Science was…”

### **The Twitter Pitch (Or Elevator Pitch)**

You should be able to get your point across in under 140 characters (ideally 120 so others can share it)
This calls for you to focus on selling what’s important.
### **The Pixar Pitch **

This is the use of a “story” to convince your buyer. Stories stick more in people’s heads as compared to resonated facts.

The Guide to every great story
  - Once upon a time ____. Every day, ____. One day ____. Because of that, ______. Because of that, ____. Until Finally _____

Finding Nemo Example:

***Once upon a time,**** there was a widowed fish, named Marlin, who was extremely protective of his only son, Nemo.*
***Every day,**** Marlin warned Nemo of the ocean’s dangers and implored him not to swim far away.*
***One day ****in an act of defiance, Nemo ignores his father’s warnings and swims into the open water.*
***Because of that,**** he is captured by a diver and ends up in the fish tank of a dentist in Sydney.*
***Because of that**** Marlin sets off on a journey to recover Nemo, enlisting the help of other sea creatures along the way.*
***Until finally,**** Marlin and Nemo find each other, reunite and learn that love depends on trust.*

Marking Data Scientist Example

**Once upon a time**, there was a marketing agency, that aimed to bridge the gap between data science and marketing.

**Every day**, the marketing team struggled to make sense of the vast amount of customer data they collected, feeling overwhelmed and unsure how to leverage it effectively.

**One day**, I joined the agency and recognized the untapped potential in the data the marketing team possessed.

**Because of that**, I approached the marketing team, offering their expertise to help them understand and harness the power of their data.

**Because of that**, I patiently explained the importance of data-driven decision-making and how data analysis could uncover valuable insights about customer behavior, preferences, and trends.

**Until finally**, I began collaborating closely with the marketing team, analyzing the data and providing actionable recommendations that enhanced their campaigns and strategies.

With each successful collaboration, the marketing team gained a deeper understanding of their audience, optimized their targeting, and achieved remarkable improvements in their marketing efforts.

Thanks to this expertise and guidance, the marketing team grew more confident in utilizing data to drive their decisions, ultimately leading to increased customer engagement, higher conversion rates, and overall business growth.

**NOTE:** As you prepare your pitch, whichever variety you choose, clarify your purpose and strategy by making sure you can answer these three questions:

1. What do you want them to know?
2. What do you want them to feel?
3. What do you want them to do?
