# When and why cover letters are important?

Cover letters are great for people who may not have the experience or technical background but have a strong passion and interpersonal skills.
A resume doesn’t really show/explain how you communicate, think, work with others, time management and other non-technical skills. These are great to explain and display in a cover letter. In a field like data science or tech, you will often get people who are very intelligent but can’t communicate with clients, managers, or teams which is a large problem. I personally hate cover letters but I know when I lacked the outside school/work projects, experience or knowledge I could make up for it in my cover letter.
At the beginning of my career, I had to bank on this a lot as I was a data scientist competing with software engineers for my 6 years in university for software engineer positions, as data science did not have its own degree with very few entry-level data science positions.
In all careers, some sort of communication is needed but in data science, it is really important as you will be explaining these very complex tasks to people who have no idea. As the “scientist” in data science, you will often be communicating the inference of your experiments thus being able to communicate to all skill levels is very important.
Therefore, it gives you a chance when you lack some technical/analytical required skills.

# Cover Letter Checklist

- Matching Header** - It should have the same header/design as your resume (so they match)
- One Full Page** - Try to fill the whole page and not more than a page
- Selling yourself as a product** - Talk about what you bring to the table
- Add passions
ie Creating an AI that understands empathy and feelings, expression is what motivates you
  - How you discover data science
- What benefits would you bring to a team?
- Paragraph 1** - Research
  - About the company
  - About the job
  - Why you are interested in this position
  - Why you are interested in this team/employer
  - Company knowledge
- Paragraph 2 - ** Matching job post requirements
  - What they are looking for and what skills do you have then add impact numbers
  - Overview of your previous experience
- Paragraph 3 - **Personal / Behavioural
  - Communication
  - Time Management
  - Teamwork
  - Leadership
  - Etc
NOTE: I personally always make this part longer than others because my resume, GitHub and LinkedIn show more of my tech skills. People often think the middle paragraph 2 should be the biggest
- Paragraph 4 ** - Summary
  - Why you’re a badass
  - Why you are a great candidate
  - Why you are really interested in this position
  - What you can help them with
  - Thank them
  - Mention you are excited to meet them in an interview
- Closing salutation
  - Closing ( E.g. “Thanks,” “Kind regards,” “Sincerely,” etc)
  - Signature (Electronic is fine)
  - Your Name
