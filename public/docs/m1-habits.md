# Why Are Habits So Important

People think they decide everything they do. Unfortunately, that’s not the case, habits account for 40% of our daily behavior.

How successful or unsuccessful you are, depends on your habits. How happy or unhappy you are, depends on your habit. How fit or unfit you are, depends on your habits. Your financial situation depends on your habits.

Adopting good habits is important for various reasons. Some of them include:

## **Good habits help us to reach our goals faster**

Habits do not necessarily dictate our success but good habits stir up the rate at which we get to achieve our goals.

For example, practicing coding for 30 minutes daily will help you reach your data science goals faster. You will solve coding problems faster and become an ideal candidate for most job recruiters.

I remember when I watched an interview with [Micheal Phelps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Phelps) (the most decorated Olympian of all time with a total of 28 medals), the interviewer asked him what do you think was the separator between him and other world-class swimmers, and he responded “Sundays.”

“A typical swimmer swims 6 days and takes off a day. That 1 extra day a week put me years ahead of my competition”Well, that got me thinking…

If we do the math…

1 day extra * 52 weeks in a year * 24 years, the length of his career = 1248 days more than his competition

=> 1248  days / 365 = **3.42** years

So quite literally, he was years ahead of his competition because of building up a simple habit which is swimming on **Sundays!**

## **Good habits improve the quality of our lives**

Positive habits gradually improve your life.

Take a minute and self-reflect on a habit you’d like to include in your routine. For example, you can plan to do 30 push-ups or bicycle crunches after lunch daily.

This is a positive habit that can bear good results. Your brain will be more active and as a result, you can accomplish more. Let’s not forget that you will be more fit.

## **Good habits put you in a position to help other people around you**

Good habits allow us to empathize with others. Remember you can’t be good enough to someone else if you are not good enough for yourself first.

The mouth speaks what flows from the heart and the heart is guided by what we feed our minds

How you help others whether in words or actions is dependent on who you are as a person. Your habits determine who you are!

## **Good habits help to boost your confidence**

It feels good when you break your bad habits and replace them with good habits.
Your confidence increases tremendously when you build a new habit and stick to it.

E.g. When you practice coding every day and someone gives you a coding problem, you are willing to face it with more confidence.

When you have practiced interview questions in advance, it gives you the confidence to face the interview panel and job recruiters.

# What Are Habits

A habit is a subconscious routine of behavior or a regular occurrence of a behavior.

Just because a habit is subconscious, doesn’t mean it’s inevitable. Good habits can be formed and bad habits can be broken.

Stick on I’m going to guide you through that.

It takes 21 days to build a habit.

When you do “a task” every day for 21 days, you will find yourself doing the same task unaware on the 22nd day going on. This is because a habit has already been formed.

It takes 90 days for the habit to become a lifestyle. This is when you start to notice changes in your life.

Others start to notice the changes in your life after 180 days of practicing the habit daily.

# Motivation vs Discipline

With the above information, you can already figure out that if you want to form a habit and make it your lifestyle, you must be disciplined.

# Habit Techniques

## 7 Habits Of Highly Effective People

- Be proactive**

Dr. Stephen Covey of the 7 habits of effective people says, “I am not a product of my circumstances, I am a product of my decisions.”

There are two types of people in life. The proactive and the reactive.

Proactive people take full responsibility for their lives. They don’t blame circumstances, conditions, or physical environment for their behavior.

They know how to control their behavior and their decisions. They take initiative and are solution-oriented. They focus their energy on what they can do or influence.

Their kind of language goes from “I can, I will, what can I do,” etc.

Reactive people on the other hand find external sources to blame for their behavior.

If the environment or the external conditions are good, they feel good, otherwise, it affects their attitude and performance.

The language revolves around words like, “I can’t, do I have to, If only,” etc.

They focus their energy on things they have little or no control over. E.g national debt, recession, etc.

Focusing your energy on things you have little or no control over will have you forgetting about yourself and important aspects of your life.

My top-tier value is self-love. Before dealing with everything else, I find ways to make myself and my environment better.

- Have the end in mind**

Have a clear vision and mission statement. Create a detailed strategy on how you plan to achieve all your goals.

If you don’t have a clear vision and SMART goals, then you allow circumstances and people to define and have control over you.

- Put first things first**
How clear are your priorities? Have you defined your values and principles? What objectives have you set for your goals?

Allow your life to be guided by principles and values and not by external forces. Have a plan to execute your goals, otherwise, you will be reacting to urgencies and unplanned agendas

- Think win-win**
Seek mutual benefit in all human interactions. If you want to go fast, go alone, if you want to go far, go together.

The goal is not to go fast and blindly, the goal is to work as a team and everyone wins. The pie is big enough for everyone to have a bite.

Help others achieve their dreams as you aim to achieve yours.

- Seek first to understand, then to be understood**
Most people listen with the intention to reply and not to understand. Be intentional!

Empathize with others by deeply understanding their needs and do not be quick to add your opinions

- Synergize**
What you are struggling with, is what someone else is good at. Be open-minded and collaborate with teams if you want to grow.

You will be shocked at how much you can learn from people. Everyone has a different perspective on how they view things. Another person’s perspective might be a better solution to a problem.

- Sharpen the saw**
What good will overworking yourself and neglecting other aspects of your life bring?

Your body is like a saw. If you use it without constantly sharpening(relaxing) it, it becomes blunt(burnt-out).

Create a work-life balance. Take care of yourself. Allow your body to relax and recharge.

## How To Build A New Habit

James Clear in his book Atomic habits teaches us how to build habits and make them stick.

The first step to forming new habits is:

### **Start with an incredibly small habit**

Do you know why most people struggle to form habits? They pick up big habits and within a day or two, they will start looking for motivation and drive/willpower to do it.

Willpower is not a skill, it’s a mental muscle. When you use your willpower, you will be self-deprecated or ego-deflated and have a hard time with the resistance of other things

What will happen on such days? You will pass! That takes you back to square 0. Remember for a habit to form, it must be repeated for at least 21 days.

Pick a new and easy habit that you don’t need the motivation to do it.

Instead of running a whole 1km marathon, you can start by running 200m every day.

### **Increase your habits in small ways**

Instead of overwhelming yourself with big tasks all at once, focus on starting small and gradually improving.

Those 1% increments will in no time increase your motivation and willpower and land you 100%. The same applies to declines.

### **Break habits into smaller milestones**

Building habits is not an easy task of course. It can get easier when you divide your goal into small manageable objectives.

If you want to run 200m daily, you can divide that into two. You do 100m in the morning and 100 in the evening.

### When you slip, bounce back quickly

It’s okay to fall, but what you do after falling is what matters.

Ask yourself what bumps could prevent your habits from happening. Plan for such occurrences and put in place measures that will help you bounce back.

With habits, you just need to be consistent, and not perfect.

### Be patient, and stick to a pace you can sustain

Consistency beats talent and professionalism. No matter what you are trying to be good at, be patient and consistent.

The grass doesn’t get all green in a day. You must consistently water it and patiently wait for the results. The same applies to your habits.

## 4 Stages Of A Habit

### CUE

A cue is a signal sent to the brain to initiate behavior.
Science has proven that all habits are triggered into at least one of the following categories:
- Location
- Time
- Emotions state
- Other people
- Following an action

A cue is the first indication that you are close to a reward, that’s why it leads to a craving. Cues are meaningless until they are interpreted.

### CRAVINGS

These are the motivational forces behind every habit. It is some sort of motivation or desire that makes us crave or want to act in a certain manner.

You don’t crave the habit, you crave the results of the habit. You have to interpret a cue for it to become a craving. The feelings, the emotions, the imaginations, are what develop the cravings.

### RESPONSE

This is the habit itself. Can occur in form of thought or action. A response will occur depending on how motivated you are or on how much friction is associated with behavior.

A response also depends on your ability. If you want to solve an MLE problem and you have no knowledge or ability to do so, the problem will never be solved.

### REWARD

The response results in a reward. We chase the rewards because they either teach us or satisfy us.

## How To Break Bad Habits

- Be aware of your triggers**
Change your environment, it will help you change your habits.

Be aware of triggers that cause bad habits. If you’re addicted to Netflix, you can avoid the sitting room or the entertainment room. If you are addicted to cookies, you can take an alternative route to avoid your bakery.

- Replace/substitute bad habits with good ones**
Plan for failure. Plan what actions you’re going to take if you get tempted to do your bad habit.

You can use the “If-then” strategy. For example, “If my friends ask to buy me fast food, then I will invite them over we make homemade food.”

Other examples of habits you can replace include
**Netflix Marathons** ====> **Sleep**
**Fast Food**  =========> **Home-Made Food**
**Toxic Friends** =======> **Mentors**
**TV** ===============> **Exercise**
**Complaining**  =======> **Gratitude**
**Overthinking**  =======> **Taking Action**
**Blame** ============> **Responsibility**

- Find an accountability partner**
How many times have you tried to break a bad habit in private? It is so easy to fail right?

For example, if you are trying to break the habit of taking fast foods and no one else knows, you will fall and give yourself an excuse when you get tempted. It is so easy to compromise your desires and thoughts.

When you join hands with another friend, you will fear, “What if my friend finds me taking fast food?” It will push you some steps back

- Surround yourself with people with the same goal as you or people who live the way you’d like to live.**
It is said that if you surround yourself with 5 billionaires, you will be the 6th. If you surround yourself with 5 beggars, you will be the 6th.

- Use the word “but” to overcome negative self-talk.**
It is so easy to look down upon yourself if you slip or fall.

If you don’t feed your mind with positive thoughts, you might give up easily. Whenever a negative thought clicks, use “but” to replace it with something positive.

“I have not managed 50 push-ups today but I am grateful I did 30, tomorrow I will make it to 50”

“I am so unfit and out of shape but I will be fit in a few months”

“I was not able to solve the coding problem without referring but I will do it the next time I try”

Here are some questions to help you get started. Keeping a track of your bad habits can help you break them easily.

- When does your bad habit actually happen?
- How many times do you do it each day?
- Where are you?
- Who are you with?
- What triggers the behavior and causes it to start?
## Habit Stacking

This is the act of following one habit with another habit. It promotes a consistent flow of habits. It helps you to build new habits and make them stick especially because your old habits are already stuck.

Associate a new habit with an old and good habit. If you want to start coding everyday for 30 minutes, you can associate it with your morning coffee.

That way, every time you take coffee in the morning, it reminds you that you should be practicing coding next.

The formula used for habit stacking is; After/Before (Current habit), I will (New habit)
I work for long hours but after I close my laptop for a break, I will do 10 push-ups.

Create a routine this way and it will make all your habits stick. Habit stacking triggers the brain on what habit to act on next after you are done with the previous.

After I take my morning coffee, I will practice coding
After I practice coding, I will do meditation
After I meditate, I will start learning a new course
After learning a new course, I will break for lunch.
After lunch, I will start working on my new project
After working on my new project, I will practice an interview question
## Implementation Intention

This is  a plan you have in advance about when and where to act in order to implement a particular habit.

The formula for this is; When situation X arises, I will perform response Y
It is a self-regulatory strategy.
## Quadrant 2

This is a time management matrix that we can use to ensure that habits stick. It helps us to know what’s urgent and what’s important in our schedules and when is the best time to practice your new habit.

There are 4 steps to owning a quadrant 2:
- Define your roles. Define your daily roles and old habits
- Goals. Define the habits you want to build
- Planning. What steps will you take to ensure that your new habit is formed and that it sticks? Break it down
- Daily adapting. How, where, and what time will you include your new habit?

# How To Reprogram Your Habits

## Understand your needs

Find habits that fulfill the needs in your life, the most important aspects of your life. Write down at least 2 top needs.
## Find out what you can do daily to fulfill the needs you have written down

Brainstorm on the two needs you have written down and what action plans you can implement to ensure they are met.
Write down at least two things you can do to fulfill each need and what they make you feel.
## Find out what is holding you back

Everyone has challenges holding them back from fulfilling certain needs. It could be the situation you are in, negative emotions, beliefs, etc.

Write down the challenges that are stopping you from fulfilling your needs and the behavior they trigger.
## Figure out what you need to change

After you have figured out what’s holding you back, determine the measures you need to put in place in order to bounce back from the challenges and find a strategy you will adopt to implement them.

Write down what you need to change and how you need to change it in order to fulfill your needs.
