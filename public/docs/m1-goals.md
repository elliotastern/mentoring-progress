# What Are Goals?

Goals are the results we want to achieve in life. They can also be defined as the direction always to be working towards

# The Importance Of Goals

- They give us direction to work towards**.
Goals provide a clear optimal path to work towards so that we are not living aimlessly.

- Goals lead to growth**
Goals help us to progress our growth curve. You learn and keep the momentum of progress

- They help us determine priorities**
When you set precise and clear goals and define your objectives, you rarely find yourself in a fix with conflicting priorities. Setting goals and objectives help us to focus on the right things.

- Goals help us measure progress in life**
Through constant monitoring and evaluation, we get to know what direction our lives are moving towards. We can determine whether we are making any progress or we are stagnant.

# Elements Of Goals

## Constraints

These are the limitations or restrictions you might encounter while working on your goals.

Below are some questions you can ask yourself to figure out your constraints.

- How much **money **will you need to invest in this goal?
- What **technologies **(Systems) do you need to use for this goal?
- What **knowledge **(Processes) do you need to learn for this goal?
- How much **time & energy **(Competing Priorities) could you afford to put into this goal?
- What **skills** do I need to acquire?
- How much **time & energy **(Competing Priorities) could you afford to put into this goal?
- What **skills** do I need to acquire?
- What is the minimum to achieve this goal?
- What is the maximum to achieve this goal?
- What am I **not **willing to compromise to achieve this goal?
- Examples
  - Health
  - Spending time with loved ones
  - Compromise my morals or ethics
- What am I willing to compromise to achieve this goal?
- Examples
  - Socializing
  - Drinking
  - Video game
  - Netflix
  - Etc
- Who do I have to become? Who is the person I must involve? What thoughts do they have? What actions do they take? What is their persona?
- Build a buffer
- For example, I want this goal in 4 months but bumps in the road will come so let’s make it  4.5 months

## Objectives - Breaking It Down

These are the immediate outcomes of goals. They involve activities and tasks that need to be done to achieve the main goal.

To set your objectives, identify the main goal then break it into smaller manageable goals.

For example, if your goal is to land that major job position, your objectives can be narrowed down to
- Negotiating the offer
- Getting the offer
- Team fit interview
- Intermediate tech rounds
- Recruiter rounds
- Applying/networking
- Creating your portfolio

Set your objectives in a checklist format. Consider the following when setting your objectives
- How will you measure degrees of completion?
- What impact will delivering on this goal create?
- What will the immediate next steps be?

## Vision

This is the long-term impact of your goals. Can also be defined as the bigger picture or the why of your goals.

Where do you see yourself in the future? Create goals that help you achieve your vision. Your vision is meant to push you out of your comfort zone.

Break down your vision into smaller goals so that it doesn’t feel overwhelming. For example, if your vision is to become a data scientist mentor, your long-term goal could be to earn a decent amount and improve your life, and your short-term goal can be breaking into the data science industry.

# Characteristics Of Goals

These are the features that should be contained in every goal you set. These characteristics are summarized in the formula, **SMART**. Let us break it down.

**S**-Specific
**M**- Measurable
**A**-Achievable
**R**-Relevant
**T**-Time-bound

## Specific

Set goals that are clearly defined, accurate/very particular, and easy to understand.

Most people get lost in achieving their goals because they set goals that are all over the place, they can’t even define their own goals when asked.

Take time and reflect on your goals and make them so clear that when you explain them to someone else, he/she can create a clear picture of what it is that you want to achieve. This will also for other people to help you achieve those goals.

## Measurable

How will you know whether your goals have been met? How will you know you’re on the right track? Can others monitor the purpose of your goals? Indulge Key Performance Indicators** (KPIs)** to help you measure progress at different stages of your goals.

For example, if your goal is to lose 60 pounds in 3 months, you can divide that into 20 pounds a month. If you step on the weighing scale at the end of month one and you’re not minus 20 pounds, it then indicates that you might not make it to 60 at the end of month 3. Go back to the drawing book and figure out what you’re missing and work on that before moving forward.

To avoid objectivity and laxity, you can use your teammate or a close friend who won’t sugarcoat things for you for your evaluation.

## Achievable

Set goals that are within your scope and that make sense to you. Sometimes your goals seem unreal to others depending on how they look at you. If deep down you know that you can achieve something and there is no constraint, then go for it.  You just need to be disciplined, and motivated and everything else will catch up.

What I hear often is “I want to land that next job in one month” but they can only put in 10 hours a week and they are not even sure about the data science interview process.

Well, I’m not saying that it is not possible but is it probable/achievable?
You need to break a goal down to see if it makes sense.

I love the quote, “People overestimate what they can achieve in a year but tend to underestimate what they can achieve in 5 years”
## Relevant

This is where you look at your vision. Is your goal helping you to achieve that vision, the bigger picture? Will it create the impact you are looking forward to achieving?

An example of this is a story I provide later in this course, about why you want to work for a particular company. For many years of my life, it was Google or nothing for me. Meaning, that I only wanted to work for Google, or I wouldn’t be happy or classify myself as successful, then I referenced my vision and I found it was for external reasons, I cared wayyyy too much about what others thought.

So, make sure that goal is relevant to what you want in life, remember it’s your life.

## Time-bound

Your goal should have well-defined timelines. These timelines should be realistic and reasonable.

Setting objectives or smaller milestones with shorter deadlines will help you achieve more within less time. Also, remember to set a buffer room for when bumps occur.

When you set the date, this should not be a wish or a hope but a planned step-by-step process to know how you can achieve the goal by that date. If the time required for the steps doesn’t align with your end goal then the date set is not reasonable

# How To Set Your Goals

- Clarity -** Clearly define your vision, long-term goals, and short-term goals.
- The Why -** Write them down and write what each means to you. A goal should never be a one-word statement. Be detailed
- Prioritize - **Rank your goals according to priorities. Which goals are more important and which ones need to be achieved first
- Break It Down - **Divide each goal into smaller objectives
- Add Deadlines - **Set timelines for each objective
- Accountability - **Write down your accountability plan. I.e. how you are going to monitor your goals and at what stages.

If no goals come to your mind right away, that’s ok, take some time to reflect, then come back to it.

**NB:** Make sure your goals pass the SMART formula. Also, note that “70% of the goals are actually reached when you write them down.”

# How To Ensure Your Goals Are Met

## Increase The Probability Of Success

- Create a vision that **excites **and **energizes** you
- Remind yourself of your **vision **regularly (Suggestion: The first thing you read/think of when you wake up and go to sleep)
  - Yes, this is cheesy but how badly do you want it?
  - A Vision board is great for this - Tape it to your wall, make it your home screen
- Develop a system of **accountability **
  - Schedule or plan it
  - Set reminders
  - Tell a friend or loved one the goal date then ask them to keep you accountable
  - Look for a mentor
- Create an **incentive structure **to generate **positive reinforcement **around your goals
  - “If we hit this goal by this date we will go out for shawarma” (PS. Shawarma is always a good idea :) )
  - “If we hit the goal before the deadline then ___ will happen”
- Find others who have the same/similar vision and **form a group**
- Set **clear deadlines**

## Decrease Risk Of Failure

- Ensure your goals are **measurable **based on objective/standardized metrics
- Create an **enabling environment **of people and resources that make it impossible to fail
- Develop a clear line of sight on **threats** to achieving your goals and **eliminate them**
  - If you know you burn out easily or often push yourself too hard without taking proper care of yourself, make sure you have something in place to remind yourself or help you manage that risk
- Cultivate the skill set needed to **overcome adversity** (Book recommendation: Outwriting the Devil)
- Keep moving forward and adapt as you go. Sustaining **momentum **is key

Starting might be a bit hard but if you keep pushing while passionate, you will reach your long-term goal and then eventually your vision.

Professionals say that you should **avoid **setting goals further than 5 years, why?

This is because the **world changes too fast** and so do you!

What you may want in your 20s you may not want in your 30s and so on, you will change and so will your goals.

The most **efficient **time frame is to set your vision for **5 years,** have a goal for each year, and then break the year down into quarters. How will you achieve Q1?

Don’t get attached to the outcome, get married to the process.

“What you do in the dark, brings you into the light”
