MVP, an acronym for Minimum Viable Project that is popular in tech, represents a project developed with just enough features to be a working product, yet designed within the shortest time frame. # How to Gain Practical Experience With A Project

### 1) Identify a project

Look for an easy task to start with and determine what you will need to start and the results you expect to achieve. Assume you are the client asking for this project, so, don’t pick something that is too easy, challenge your mind. Pick a more tangible data science project that will add credibility to your portfolio

**2) Decide on your technical spec**
A technical spec is a checklist that outlines the technical requirements for your project. It tells you what programming language, tools, and software you’ll use.
To decide on this:
- Check the skills that are in demand in the job market.( You can do this by checking job boards like Indeed and AngelList)
- Find at least 5 job postings that interest you.
- Make a list of the technical requirements each of those jobs is asking for.
- Rank these requirements from the most commonly mentioned to the least
- Pick the top 30% to 40% of these skills and make them your checklist for your project

**Note**: Don’t make your project about these skills only. Remember, the reason you are creating your project is to demonstrate your ability to get the work done, which is what impresses employers.
### 3) Get the data

The next step is to gather the data you will use for your project. You can research existing datasets like Kaggle or build your own using web-scraping tools. I recommend collecting your own data because it:
- Makes your project unique.
- Demonstrates your ability to fulfill the needs companies/employers are looking for.
These two make you stand out in the competitive job market.

**Note:** Do not change the problem to fit the available dataset, collect data that is ideal for your project.

If you use publicly available datasets, [here](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qPo7o34JD0kANy2MuEHk9h1q_GuhFcdONJLxnT00Mf0/edit?usp=drive_link) is a helpful guide with screenshots on how to navigate Kaggle to get the best dataset. The guide also has notebooks from other data scientists for inspiration.

You can collect data in three different ways:
- You can use APIs (most common)
- You can scrap websites
- You can privately message people who have access to private datasets
Here are great [lists](https://github.com/public-apis/public-apis) of publicly available APIs you can check for inspiration.
You can also check this [list](https://github.com/sharpest-minds/hiring-tips/blob/master/projects.md) of SharpestMind’s past projects.
### 4) Preprocess the data

Now that you have your data, start preparing it to solve the problem. Take your time to clean and preprocess it. Identify every area of insufficiency; any incorrect or unclear labels, unbalanced audio clips, low-quality images, etc.
Now brainstorm ways in which you will remove what’s not needed, add what’s needed, and adjust what’s hanging.
For example, if you need to add new high-quality images, add new clear labels, or adjust audio clips, do that.
### 5) Set a clear goal

After you’re done preprocessing your data, set clear goals and objectives.
How will you use the data you have to solve the problem? How will you know that you are on the right track? What will inform your way forward? Remember, this is your project, and you are responsible for every stage of it, if you don’t involve your mentor or fellow mentees, you have to figure everything out yourself.
You need to avoid simple errors and false positives at all costs. In short, prioritize accuracy at all levels. Clear goals and objectives and constant monitoring will help you get the hang of this.

### 6) Review the literature

Let’s be honest, building projects, and especially doing it all on your own, is not easy. It is okay to look at how other data scientists have solved similar problems. You can search for other publications on arxiv.org, or paperswithcode.com.
If you can tweak an existing project model, go for it. If you must build one from scratch, then go for it. All in all, I recommend you try both ways and see which one works best. That way you will avoid reinventing the wheel.
### 7) Implement the solution

Once you have the right data, clear goals, and the best model for your project, you can now work on implementation. You only need to ensure that your implementation meets the end-user’s requirements. For example, if your project aims to develop a mobile app, verify that your model is small enough to fit all smartphones. If it needs to run in real-time, determine the requirement for that. Also, show the compromises you would have to make for the app to reach its full potential, and how those compromises would affect the quality of your model’s output.
You can work on building a proof of concept, as most times it is enough, you only need to have a clear direction to optimize performance.
This last step to production could require months of tuning in real life

### 8) Share your work

You have everything ready and your project is finally ready to solve the problem you were aiming for. Do not keep it to yourself. Use it as a gateway to land your dream job by showcasing it to other colleagues, potential collaborators, and future employers.

You can:
- Share your code on GitHub
- Share it on LinkedIn
- Write a blog post

Describe your thought process, the challenges you faced, how you overcame them, and the entire process of building the project from start to finish. Do this in a way that sells your services to potential employers. Remember, your portfolio adds to your experience.

Each project adds to your experience and puts your machine learning knowledge to use. The more projects you work on, the more experience you gain, and the stronger your portfolio gets.
### TIP: Don't let the perfect be the enemy of the good

Perfectionism can hinder you from recognizing the great progress you have made on your project. You don’t have to get everything right, especially for your first projects. You only need to get good enough and keep improving as you create more projects.
To get good enough, recognize the “must-haves” and the “nice-to-haves.”
Ensure you start your project with the “must-haves” so that if you’re out of time, you can make compromises on the “nice-to-haves.” the other reason I recommend you start with the most important aspects is so that you can leave some time for mock interviews at the end of the mentorship.
So, as a general rule in data science, start with building a data pipeline for your project first. With a data pipeline, you can develop the first end-to-end version of your project and fine-tune each component later. This saves you from having to learn how to use a tool from scratch when time is running out.
Real industry projects are time-constrained so if you follow this tip, employers will see that you know how to prioritize the aspects with the highest value in your projects, this increases your chances of getting picked.

# Questions to brainstorm a project for what could be included in an end-to-end project?

The worksheet at the end of this module ([here](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1r6UhULDdti65n_IpBQbUJ3mVtwrUVKctJQEMSHvB3EA/edit?usp=sharing)) will have the following questions plus more to brainstorm a project for this mentorship.
- Do you want to do it solo or with other mentees (if applicable)?
- What are your interests/hobbies/passions?
- Are there any of your interests that you could use to solve a problem (doesn’t have to be a new breakthrough idea)? It is better to pick an idea that interests you to stay motivated and driven by it.
- What is the problem we are trying to solve?
- Is the problem descriptive, diagnostic, predictive or prescriptive analytics?
  - Descriptive **- describes what happened in the past
  - Diagnostic **- Describes why something happened in the past
  - Predictive** - Predicts what is most likely to happen in the future
  - Prescriptive **- Suggests actions you can take to affect the predicted outcomes
- Does the project include machine learning? If yes, then what subtopic of machine learning are you interested in? (ie Computer Vision (CV), Natural Language Process (NLP), Classification, Regression, Time Series, etc)
- What type of data collecting needs to be done for the problem? It is recommended to have an original dataset by either collecting/scraping yourself or combining multiple datasets.
- Are you doing supervised learning or unsupervised learning? If supervised, is the data labelled? If not, then how do you plan on labelling it?
- What are the must-have technical skills for this project? If you are having trouble with this then go to a job board and find 5 job applications that you are interested in. Take those technical aspects and try to incorporate 30% - 40% of them.
- Does the project include dashboards? What dashboard software will you use? (ie Power BI, Tableau, Kibana, etc)
- Will the project be hosted in the cloud? If yes, what cloud platform (ie Azure, Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud Platform, etc)?
