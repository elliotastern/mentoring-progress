# “But why would I maximize my LinkedIn?”

If you have a **Complete Profile** then there is **more info about you** which means the **more your profile shows up in search/suggestions, **the more LinkedIn’s algorithm will find **similarities for potential connections** which leads to **higher odds** of:
- Referrals
- Recruiters
- Clients
- Collaborations
- The potential people that will help you out
- Jobs

I consider LinkedIn and networking like investing. The earlier you start depositing a little each month the earlier the compound growth starts leading to exponential growth in the long term.

Continuing the investing analogy, investing $10 a day or $300 a month into an index fund like the S&P 500, will gain you over $1 Million over 40 years starting at the age of 25 but if you just wait ten years at the age of 35 then the difference of $700,000! That’s the power of exponential growth.

This is the same as your LinkedIn and networking if you spend 10 min a day improving, connecting or posting material.

I started ~ 5 years ago with the bare minimum information but this allowed me to add coworkers, friends, and people I met. After really focusing on it in mid-2021 I am at the point where my network works for me even though I don’t post content. I do regret not utilizing the power of a network earlier, for my previous roles. Now if I did post content (which is my current goal to do more) then the outcome I get from LinkedIn and my network will continuously grow exponentially.
In conclusion, maximizing LinkedIn = Using every feature on LinkedIn = more help in your career, life and much more.
Social media is a long-term game for your brand (you). You want to create meaningful connections, you want to provide meaningful content.

# Why You Should Brand Yourself On LinkedIn

Your company doesn't control your LinkedIn brand.

And here are 7 reasons why they never should:

1. Your brand compounds instead of starting over with each job.
2. Your brand stays with you regardless of where you work.
3. Your brand develops connections, not transactions.
4. Your brand becomes your resume over time.
5. Your brand unlocks new opportunities.
6. Your brand isn't controlled by HR.
7. Your brand is your safety net.

Don't build your brand around the company you work for.

They won't help you rebuild it when you're let go.

# LinkedIn Profile - Checklist

- Picture **
  - Should be 80% of your shoulders and above
  - There was a study (I think by Harvard) that showed how a smile in your profile picture brings more traffic to your page because it makes people feel welcome and want to work with you. Thus, I highly suggest your picture has a smile.
  - The picture should be within 2 years of yourself (so they say)
  - Not distracting background - There are lots of free apps to remove the background of pictures if you are looking for that.
  - Looking straight into the camera
  - Ideally Professional
  - Choose Option “All LinkedIn Members” Or “Public” to allow search engines to find your profile easier
- Header Picture (Max 10 mins - Don’t overthink this)**
  - This is free space to add more words as there is a maximum amount of letters that can be used in every other section
  - Quotes or your values are great as they tell others who you are as a person
The best example I have seen:

- Headline - Use **[Free LinkedIn Headline Analyzer](https://cultivatedculture.com/headline-analyzer/)** **
  - Uncheck “Show current Company in my intro” (unless big company)
  - Uncheck “Show education in my intro” (Unless top tier)
  - Affirmative words
  - Remove “aspiring ____” even if you are not in the field yet. Just because you don’t have the job title doesn’t mean you don’t have the skills. (i.e. “Aspiring Data Scientist” => “Data Scientist”)
  - Don’t just put your job title or degree (Your profile already shows what school and your employer, no need to repeat it.
  - And honestly, I really don’t like when people put the school or employer because it tells me that you care about the status and it’s not the school or employer you work for that makes you special, **IT’S YOU!**
  - You are more than your job title or the company you work for!
  - Remember it's not the degrees, certificates, job title, salary or the company you work for that makes you special and unique. It's you! So write what makes you unique.
  - The goal is to be as general as possible to reach as many searches you can but also be as specific to clearly tell your audience what you do or who you are. If you can add an impact number in your one-liner, even better! Use:
  - TEMPLATE 1: [Title] / Position] | Helping [industry] [problem you solve] by [explain process] | [add keywords].
  - TEMPLATE 2: [Keywords] | [Skills] | [Results-focused Value Proposition]

  - Data Scientist | Python, R, Tableau | I help Hospitals Use Big Data To Reduce Readmission Rates By 37%
- “Open to”**
This section is **very important!** There are two modes, one that the public can see and one only that recruiters can see.
This mode should **ALWAYS BE ON** even if you are not currently looking for a position! Why? Because when your profile is optimized enough you won’t ever have to apply again, recruiters or hiring managers will just come to you. Then even if a position doesn’t interest you, it’s a great live practice for interviews and negotiations. Which will lead to you feeling more comfortable with those stages later on when you are looking for a position and actually like a position. But also if you can guarantee 100% that your employer will never 1) Go out of business 2) Lay you off 3) fire you 4) not value you and 5) be unsatisfied with the employer then fine you have convinced me and you can turn it off :)

# How to know if your profile is optimized?

If you have optimized your LinkedIn profile enough when LinkedIn sends you the notification “You appeared in X amount of searches this week”.
Click it and scroll down you will see you will see the top 3 keywords people searched for you. You can also do this by going to your portfolio in the Analytics section.

Click the “search appearances” then scroll down and see the keywords (typically only shows one here). You can also see what type of people are searching for you. If you are a particular industry/domain/sector you are looking for try to tailor it to that!
