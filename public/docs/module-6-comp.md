# Why Should You Negotiate The Complete Compensation Package?

- Compensation packages can save you the cost of some necessities such as medical insurance
- It can help you maintain a healthy work-life balance. If you negotiate perks such as paid vacation days, sick days, and hybrid or remote roles, then you get time to work and take care of yourself.
- Some of the benefits in the compensation package can help you prepare for the future. E.g. sponsored training can help you get a promotion. 401(k) can help you prepare for retirement, Etc.
# What Is A Complete Compensation Package

It refers to all the ways that the company/employer pays the employees. Includes basic salary, bonuses, insurance, vacations, etc.
When negotiating salaries it is important to do background research and find all the possible perks you could get in addition to your base salary.
It helps you make an informed decision on whether to close the offer or find a better employer that will support your requirements. It is the possible perks your employer offers in addition to your base salary.
It helps you make an informed decision on whether to accept the offer or find a better employer who will support all your requirements.
# The 3 Most Important Concepts In Negotiation

- You should ALWAYS negotiate!**
No matter how good you feel about the salary, negotiate! I made the mistake of not negotiating only to end up as the lowest-paid employee in my department
- Know your value**
Even before you start the negotiation process, have in mind what your value is and what you want to come out with

- Go higher than you think you can get**
Never give a minimum value while negotiating.

# Types Of Compensation With Value

- Equity or Stocks Share**
  - This is not ideal if you are looking for a short-term role. Often, the company offers a small amount at first and after years, you get the rest.
  - It can be really good if you get it upfront though. (Assuming they are a big company or consistently growing long-term)
- Pension Plan**.
- Employer contributions match** - the company matches the amount of money you put in your retirement savings account.
- Health/dental benefits**
  - Please make sure you confirm with your employer what it includes and whether it includes dependants and partners.
- Number of days for vacation**
- (Paid) Sick days**
- Parental leave days**
- Sponsor my career development or Allowance**
  - This can be a useful add-on if the pay is lower and you have a planned course you want to take.
  - Having the specifics on the cost and timing of the course will help
- Ergonomic equipment**
- Office Supplies or Equipment**
- Severance** - A payment if the company goes bankrupt or cutbacks
  - Suggested for start-ups
- Signing Bonus**
  - In the tech and data field, it is common to ask for a signing bonus from anywhere between $5k - $20k
  - There was a trending post (at the time I wrote this) that was going around about a $100k signing bonus for an entry-level LOL so you never know
  - Signing bonuses are great if you don’t plan to stay there long term but not great if staying long-term as your salary increase will be based on your base salary, thus it’s better to get that to increase the base
- Paid Phone and/or plan**

# Types Of Compensation Without Value (A.K.A Benefits)

- Title
  - Personally, this means nothing to me but I hear/read it often so I thought I would include it
  - It is not legally binding like your name so I don’t understand the big deal, just change it when on LinkedIn or resume LOL
- Internal hack-a-thons
- Free snacks, coffee, beverages, lunches, etc
- Paid or free parking
- Remote working
- Remote working (World Wide)
- Unpaid vacation
- Flexible work hours
- Evaluation Period - How frequently compensation is reviewed (E.g. 6-months, annually, etc)
  - For example, there was a company in Canada that would sign recent graduates but put in their contract no pay raise until after 5 years with the company
- Evaluation Reason - Can compensation be changed on tasks or responsibility /achievements or only time?
- Inflation adjustment
- Internal promotions over external hires
- Travel included
- Office with a __
  - E.g. window, a door
- A say in projects and tasks you work on
- A mentor to learn from

  - If you’re being reasonable, using data and backing up your points, why would they extract the offer
- You Don’t Want To Seem Greedy**

“How much would the complete compensation package be?

Example

- Sick days => 2-5 days worth of pay => ~$1500
- Vacation Pay => 2-3 weeks of pay

- Stock option => ~$3k /year

- Total Compensation Package => ~$89,900

According to psychology, don’t choose a rounded-off number (e.g. $95000) instead, choose $94750 because when people hear/read that they think it must have been a reason why, which makes it more reasonable.

# Red Flags In An Employer/Offer:

Just because there are red flags, doesn’t mean that the employer isn’t trying to change or that you shouldn’t continue the application process. You could always use them as leverage Hehe

# What If It’s Too Late In Compensation Negotiation?

By now you've heard me say that you should not share your salary expectations during your interviews. It's sort of my mission to make sure everyone knows about The Dreaded Salary Question because it's where folks make the most common and biggest salary negotiation mistake.

**What if you already told them your salary expectations?**

Here's another version of it:

**I would like to ask, how do I negotiate a base salary increase + equity in such a way that I am not losing money? Should I show them my total compensation package?**

Here's another version of it:

**The employer knows my current salary but not the expected one. Can I still negotiate for a higher salary or will they be biased based on my current salary?**

And another one:

**I revealed my salary expectations. Is there a way to come back from that, if a job is offered? So how do you recover if you've already disclosed your salary expectations?**

As with most good questions, the answer is "It depends..." In this case, it depends on how early you are in the interview process when you catch this mistake:

**Definitions:**
Early in the process—You haven't started your formal interviews yet
Late in the process—You already have a job offer

**1. You haven't started your formal interviews yet**

There's still time!

The problem you need to overcome is that most companies start by thinking, "What's the minimum we need to offer this person to get them to take the job?"

Unfortunately, they have a simple formula to find that "minimum" number they need to offer: your salary expectations plus a little more to entice you to make the leap.

You want to switch their thinking to, "What do we have to offer this person to convince them to take the job?"

**See the difference?**

In the first one, they're looking for the smallest number they can get away with. In the second one, they're looking for a number big enough to convince you to make the leap. These are probably two very different numbers.

**How do you change their thinking?**

At every stage of the interview process, your primary goal is to tell a story about how their company will be better with you on their team. Just keep telling that story over and over again.

Every time they ask you a question, think,** "How can I answer this question in a way that adds another chapter to the story about how their company will be better if they bring me on board?"**

If you do change their thinking from "minimum number" to "number big enough to be persuasive", they may forget that you told them your salary expectations and focus on making a compelling offer instead.

That may be enough to break away from your stated salary expectations, but what if you try this and still get an offer that's based on that information?

2.** You already have a job offer**

You may have experienced something like this:

You got a job offer and felt great because it was more than you were currently making. Then, a few minutes later you thought, "Wait a minute. This offer is just my current salary plus 5%! Is that a coincidence?"

Nope, it's not a coincidence :(

Once you get this far, it's much harder to recover and drastically improve your offer. But you have a couple of options based on your own assessment of your minimum acceptable salary:

If a counter of 10-20% above their offer is above your minimum acceptable salary, then that's your best move. You could negotiate more than your minimum or you can fall back to your minimum as your line in the sand (your walk-away number).

If that won't work, you can use this technique to negotiate a lowball job offer—ask them if they can make improvements to the offer so you can consider them. This is your way of letting them know that they need to switch their thinking from "minimum" to "most convincing".

Your options are slim, but you do have options even if you've already made the most common salary negotiation mistake!
# To Know/Remember About Asking (For More)

- It is Normal** - You might be nervous but to an HR/Recruiter/manager, it is normal to talk about money
  - It’s in their job description to discuss this
  - Talking about money is **normal** because we work for money and we need money to survive
  - If a manager is uncomfortable with talking about THAT, that’s their issue
- Always prepare**
  - Practice your ask
  - leverage the social proof (data and statistics)
- Always research**
- Not A Request or Gift** - Asking for more is not a request or gift it is a measure of your confidence and skills
- Avoid sounding greedy/threatening/nervous **
- Don't ask directly** - “How can we build a path to ___”
  - “...including an extra week of vacation”
  - “...move closer to $98000”
  - It puts less pressure on them and won’t feel like such a threat
- "Upon reflection"** - is a great professional transition to show your proof
- “Based on Market Value”** - Is another way to say unvalued
- Never apologize **- Justify your offer but don’t **EVER **apologize
  - If someone doesn’t see your value then mutually agree to the breakup
  - You don’t need to prove it to them
- Salary Structure** - A lot of companies have a salary structure built in thus they can’t put you above it. Sometimes this is great for asking for a signing bonus or other benefits
- Don’t say your minimum**
- Goal: Disclose salary expectations to the company after receiving an offer.

# Questions To Indirectly Ask About Compensation

- How do you decide when and how to promote or increase pay in the company? Do you do the evaluations annually, time-dependent, or by performance results?
- What growth opportunities do you offer in the company? (E.g. promotions, long-term plans, etc?)
- When high-level positions are available, do you consider internal employees first?

# How To Handle Multiple Offers

“Jaret, you told me to aim for 4 offers, now my #2 is offering me the job but I don’t know whether to accept it because I’m still interviewing with #1 and it’s going well. What should I do?”

I hear you!

I need you to understand that you are the CEO of your own life. Your job is to think about what will be best for YOU.

Personally, I would accept the offer from my #2 (assuming I had already shared my other offer and there’s a deadline I can’t extend)
Then I would continue interviewing at my #1

If I don’t get the job at my #1, then I still have an option to fall back on.

If I get the job at my #1, and I have analyzed the info and I want to stay with the #, I will politely decline the offer with my top choice.

When I give this advice, people naturally say, "but you gave them your word!"

"You're going to burn bridges!"

Maybe.

Probably.

But I've never seen a company save someone from a layoff because they passed over their dream job to "keep their word."

One year the company is all about how “we are a family” The next year 500+ people are laid off. Do they fear burning bridges, absolutely not.

Should you fear to do the same?

As I mentioned earlier, if the employer doesn’t care about you, you shouldn’t either.

Make the decision that’s best for YOU.
# Jaret’s Activity For Preparing For The Ask Or An Uncomfortable Situation

**Tip: **When you plan to ask for more either through contract negotiation or for a raise you want to prepare as much as possible. An activity I do for difficult situations, I plan for how the conversation is going to go. I typically use a mind map to get all my fears, worries, thoughts, and ideas out then I try to make it a step-by-step plan.

Some of the questions are more for general negotiations so I will include an example below.

- Gather your needs, wants, and nice-to-haves from earlier
- Do your research
- Get your proof
  - “Why do I deserve this much”
  - “What are my skills and experience for that value”
  - “What objections could I get?”
  - etc
- Write out your ask  (View the [BONUS](https://drive.google.com/file/d/16oAgtOsgU01taxvLU2aw2RBhfB2IYtoq/view?usp=sharing) email script for additional help)
- Think of all the questions they will ask you, then prepare the answers
- Think of all the questions you will ask
- Plan the process/frame
- Then branch off a path for the most common answers. Then continue the branch on how you would handle that and to bring the negotiation to where you want to go next
  - Example
  - You say your ask
  - “Yes - that sounds great, I actually want to give you more”
  - “Yes - that sounds reasonable”
  - “No - I was thinking ___ (lower)”
  - “No - ARE YOU SERIOUS!? YOU ARE NOT WORTH THAT MUCH”
  - Then you branch off each of those answers and keep repeating this process

# Jaret’s Advice

You will always matter to yourself and your loved ones more than to a company.

People won’t remember your job title, salary or company when you are gone, they will remember the time spent and the memories with you.

I have personally been screwed by employers and I know too many people who have been screwed over by employers.

I want to bring the power back to equilibrium between the employer and the employee.

Just like how an employee can be replaced in an instant… well an employer can be too!

Ask yourself “How long after you die would an employer look for your placement?”

Remember, what a job is at a high level…a way to pay the bills and survive, not your identity
