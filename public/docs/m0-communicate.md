# When Should You Ask A Question

(The dichotomy of asking a question to the manager too early vs too late)
When is too early to ask a question?
When is too late to ask a question?
How long should you struggle before you ask a question?
It is important to know when to ask a question. One, it will help you save time, and second, it will prevent you from diverting from the topic.
Psychology says it takes a person on average 15 mins to completely switch topics and recall what they did last (It’s called context switching).

There are no dumb questions, but I might respond with,  “What have you tried, and what particularly are you struggling with?”
Why would I do this instead of just answering your question?
- This will provide the context and the problem while helping save time for both parties.
- I can help you in the short-term (aka answering your question) or help you in the long-term (aka helping you become resourceful and an effective communicator)
Why should you ask a question you can solve by yourself?
This is something I struggled with in my first internship, so I created this technique.

## Write It, Think It, Search It - Technique

### **Write it**

Write the question to the person you want to ask via any communication platform (social media, e-mail, slack, text, etc.) but **don’t send it.**

If you can’t put your question together, you don’t know your question! How do you expect another person to understand it?

I suggest you go back to your problem and break it into smaller problems, and then structure your question.

When you have a question, **repeat step 1**

### Search it

You can now copy your question and Google it.

If you find an answer, great, delete your question and move on with your journey.

If Google didn’t understand the context of your question and didn’t provide any useful links, how would you expect someone else to understand your question?

Go back to the drawing book and break your problem into smaller problems, when you structure a good question, repeat step 1.

If you found multiple answers to your question and you’re not sure which answer to go with, continue to step 3.

### Still Unsure?

If you’re still not sure, add context to your question and mention that you have already tried.

Provide any useful resources you found e.g. links, screenshots, etc

Then send the question.

This will allow the other person to help you more efficiently.

# How To Solve Any Problem

It is easy to feel discouraged when you don’t know how to solve a problem.
Embrace the challenge and use this technique that I believe is fit for anyone looking for a solution.

- Break your problem into smaller manageable problems**
If you can’t break down your problem, then you don’t understand it. These questions will help you narrow down to the root source.

- What is the problem?
- Why is this a problem?
- Does this problem matter if yes then why?
- What do you know?

- Change your mindset or perspective**
If you are stuck on a problem, try to view it from someone else’s view. Maybe you are missing the correct angle to view the problem and/or why it is important.

If you change your perspective and you’re still stuck, change your mindset. Feed your mind with positive thoughts and reassure yourself that you’re going to solve the problem.

There is no problem that is uncommon to man. It is likely that someone else has encountered a similar problem before and solved it and therefore you are going to solve it too.

## Feeling Overwhelmed?

It is okay to feel overwhelmed along this journey. Don’t beat yourself when it happens.

Actually, it’s inevitable on every journey. Embrace it, keep pushing, and don’t look back.

Just take a deep breath and take a step back. Find effective ways to renew your energy and get back stronger.

### BONUS: Here is a script that might help get you unstuck:

- What emotions am I feeling right now?
- Why am I feeling this way?
- Does my body need something? (ie food, sleep/caffeine, water, exercise, etc)
- Is this task too easy (aka bored) or too hard (aka discouraged)?
  - If too easy => (1) make it fun (ie a game) (2) Put a reward after completion (3) Put a punishment if not completed by X time
  - If too hard =>  Break it down into trivial steps

### BONUS: The 5 Whys- Root Cause Analysis

This is an approach invented by the Toyota production team to make the nature of problems and solutions clear.

It helps us understand the root cause of a problem and what we can do to solve the problem.

So here is how it works. Whenever you encounter a problem, ask yourself *“WHY”* 5 times. By the end of the 5th “*WHY”* you will have a clear picture of what went wrong.

These* WHYs* should be interrelated.

For example, you are invited for an interview but it didn’t start off great. Here are some WHYs that can help you see what went wrong.

| 1 | WHY | I joined the interview late |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | WHY | I woke up late |
| 3 | WHY | My alarm didn’t work |
| 4 | WHY | The battery died |
| 5 | WHY | I didn’t check the battery before I slept |

You see by the time you’re done answering what could have possibly gone wrong, you will have an idea of what caused the whole problem, and what you should have done to solve it.

This is the solution you carry with you the next time you have an interview.

# Frequently Asked Questions

- What do I do next?

What do you think you should do next?
Write down what you think you should do next if it’s a no-brainer, then take action. If not, ask if that’s the correct next step.

- How do I do ___

Sure, how do you think you should do it? What have you tried? Can you send me a loom of your progress and explain the context? What resources have you tried?

- I don’t understand ___

Sure, what do you understand about it? What have you tried? What resources have you tried? How do you think you should learn it? Break down the problem.
