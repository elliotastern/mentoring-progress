# Optimized Resume Checklist

- Header **- The header is very important but it shouldn’t take more space than needed
  - Maximize space - **Often I see resumes with their contact info, not in the header, you have a header section for a reason, maximize that inch of that paper
  - DO NOT PUT YOUR ADDRESS **-  it’s just creepy. Remember you are sending this over the internet so anyone can see it but it is also not needed. If an employer needs it then that could be a red flag. If you do really want to put it then put city, state/province, country
  - Hyperlink all your contact methods** - (E.g. Email, LinkedIn, website, Github, Blog, etc). If the URL is too long use the main word then hyperlink it. (E.g. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/example-profile/)) Only put contact methods that you use (E.g. don’t put LinkedIn if you don’t use it
  - Profession Email** - Never use your school or work email, you will eventually lose it and it is not ideal anyway. If you don’t have a personal one, create one.
- No small common mistakes** - One of the first ways to get your resume thrown away is to have a few common spelling mistakes since it looks like you did not put the effort in. Check out the list below here Common Resume Mistakes
- Spacing is consistent** - You want to maximize the space on the resume since you are limited by how much you write. To widen the margins, make the alignments to maximize
- Most important first** - They say on average it takes people 10 seconds to make a first impression, thus you should put the most impactful /relative stuff first. (ie skills, projects, education, certs, etc)
  - Relative Experience > Relative project experience > Relative Course Experience > Degree
  - Knowledge **does not** replace experience
- Always Learning** - Tech and data move very fast you will always be learning, taking more courses/certificates/etc thus I suggest you clump your Certificates and your education to save space (aka only one header)
- One or two paged resumes? **- There is an unwritten rule that if you have **5 or more** relative years of experience you can have more than 1 page. I would still cap it off at 1 though because no one wants to read a book.
- Maximize space **- if your resume is not columns you want to maximize the space horizontally.
  - Use dividers between thoughts if needed: “|” ( I recommend), “\” “~” or a bullet, a symbol
  - This is important for the Education section
- List key skills **- You should have a section with a list of key skills for quick lookup, Ideally near the top separating two sections of a longer text. Use the dividers in the above point to separate them. Write them most ___ (e.g. skill at, relevant to the job, etc)
- List technical skills **- I suggest near the bottom you write your technical skills, broken up into several categories like languages, software, packages/libraries/frameworks, models, etc. This will allow you to remove the sentences that say “develop an end-to-end ML pipeline with Python” so you can write more of the results and impact.
- MEASURABLE & IMPACTFUL Bullet points **- Each bullet point for experience or projects should be clear, and concise and tell the “how” and the result, not just the “what”. **MAKE SURE EACH BULLET HAS A “SO WHAT” WHICH IS EITHER A QUANTITATIVE VALUE OR SOME MEASURE OF IMPACT.  **(Review: [03 - How To Measure Impact](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1c_-GUG9nEFxvq7yt7zZJtOcroiEVdsH0wlJs8bviDPM/edit?usp=sharing))
  - At most 4 bullets for each position/project
  - Every sentence must have what **you **did **why **and a **“So what”** (Review: [03 - How To Measure Impact](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1c_-GUG9nEFxvq7yt7zZJtOcroiEVdsH0wlJs8bviDPM/edit?usp=sharing)) **TIP:** You can also try using [Google’s X by Y by Z formula](https://www.inc.com/bill-murphy-jr/google-recruiters-say-these-5-resume-tips-including-x-y-z-formula-will-improve-your-odds-of-getting-hired-at-google.html)– "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]”.
  - No two bullets in the same position/project can start with the same verb to start the bullet
  - ML Template:
  - Built [𝚝𝚢𝚙𝚎_𝚘𝚏_𝚖𝚘𝚍𝚎𝚕] using [𝚝𝚘𝚘𝚕𝚜_𝚞𝚜𝚎𝚍] in order to [𝚖𝚘𝚍𝚎𝚕_𝚞𝚜𝚎_𝚌𝚊𝚜𝚎] for [𝚖𝚘𝚍𝚎𝚕_𝚌𝚘𝚗𝚜𝚞𝚖𝚎𝚛], resulting in [𝚇] improvement to [𝚜𝚞𝚌𝚌𝚎𝚜𝚜_𝙺𝙿𝙸].

Example:

Built 𝚇𝙶𝙱𝚘𝚘𝚜𝚝 𝚌𝚕𝚊𝚜𝚜𝚒𝚏𝚒𝚎𝚛 using 𝙼𝙻𝙵𝚕𝚘𝚠, 𝚂𝚌𝚒𝚔𝚒𝚝-𝙻𝚎𝚊𝚛𝚗 𝚊𝚗𝚍 𝙰𝚆𝚂 𝚂𝚊𝚐𝚎𝚖𝚊𝚔𝚎𝚛 in order to predict 𝚌𝚞𝚜𝚝𝚘𝚖𝚎𝚛 𝚙𝚞𝚛𝚌𝚑𝚊𝚜𝚎 𝚙𝚛𝚘𝚋𝚊𝚋𝚒𝚕𝚒𝚝𝚢 for 𝚘𝚞𝚛 𝚖𝚊𝚛𝚔𝚎𝚝𝚒𝚗𝚐 𝚝𝚎𝚊𝚖, resulting in a 𝟸𝟶% improvement to 𝚖𝚊𝚒𝚕𝚎𝚛 𝚌𝚊𝚖𝚙𝚊𝚒𝚐𝚗 𝙲𝙿𝙰.
- Project Names **- Clear and concise. Project Name = High-Level Goal + what you did
  - Examples:
  - (Orginal) “Bike Sales (Enterprise Data Analytics)” => (New) “Bike Sales Analysis”
  - (Orginal) “Logistic Regression Analysis: College Dropout Rates” =>  (New) “College Dropout Prediction - Using Logistic Regression”

- Add links to anything you can (ie portfolio, projects, awards, certifications) **
- Don’t overuse buzz words** - We want to stand out to the ATS but not make it horrible to read for a human
- Don’t overuse the same action verbs** - see the bonus action verb list
- All information provides benefits** - Think of everything you put on your resume (or portfolio) as
Does it add benefit to being a _____ (insert job title)?
  - If **yes **=> keep it
  - if **no **=> remove it
  - if maybe => then ask yourself, if you would be impressed if someone told you they know ____ (ex. "I am a data scientist and I know MS Word")
- Stand out**
  - Use your “sell yourself as a product” worksheet as your guide for your resume
  - If you are tailoring every resume for each application (which I highly suggest against) make sure you highlight (literally! With yellow or a stand-out colour) the key results or keywords to match the words in a resume.
- Check your resume on [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/) - JobScan.co is a highly helpful tool that will tell you exactly how keyword-optimized your resume actually is and it will provide suggestions on what keywords you need to include more of.
  - On one side you will be prompted to copy and paste your resume (again another reason to keep the resume simple).
  - On the other, you will be prompted to copy and paste the job description you’re targeting (ie Data Scientist)
  - 60% to 70%+
**Warning: **
JobScan.co is a company. Like all companies, it wants to make money. It will be very rare that your resume will ever rank 90% to 100%. They will intentionally rank it low in hopes that you then purchase one of their resume-building tools. Don’t fall for it. We took care of this for you (coming up).

# VERY IMPORTANT EXAMPLE:

People who get hired at top companies understand how to measure their impact
**Resume Example #1**
•**Poor**: Was asked to manage the launch of a new software product. The launch was successful and as a result the team was acknowledged for their efforts.
•**Better:** Managed the launch of a new software product in a competitive space which led to increased users and revenue. As a result, the team was awarded a stock bonus.
•**Great:** Led the release of a new $2.9M software package that generated $1.8M in revenue, and 300k new clients within the first 5 months. All the 14 team members involved in building the software were awarded stock units in acknowledgement for their performance.
**Resume Example #2 **
**•Poor**: I was asked by the data science manager at Bell to test new data to improve productivity at the company. I found a way to communicate efficiently within the team and the management gave me a promotion.
**•Better**: I tested new data that improved communication at Bell. As a result, the team was greatly productive and all the quarterly goals assigned were met. I was recognized for my efforts and offered a promotion.
**•Great**: I tested new data that improved communication and productivity by 60%. As a result, the team reached its quarterly goal, adding $780K in revenue. As a result, I was offered my first promotion within 3 months of being in the team.
Measure what matters.

# Common Resume Mistakes

- You have spelling mistakes
- Your resume is not clear. If someone can read the first 10 lines without knowing what you do, it’s not clear
- You have used a font that is not easily readable.
- The formatting is inconsistent
- There’s so much text that makes it difficult to read through the important parts
- You have sent the exact same resume more than once
- You have included your picture.
- Submitting your resume in a format that’s not PDF
- You have a second page that’s only 1 sentence long. It should be either 1 page or 1.5- 2 pages
- Weird or inconsistent spacing
- Including academic publications that are not related to the role you’re applying to
- Inconsistent punctuation
- Using more than one date format
- Unexplained gaps for more than 3 months
- Work experience that’s not organized chronologically
- Adding “communication skills” “time management skills” or any other soft skills that’s easy to claim but hard to assess in an interview
- Adding Excel, Word, or PowerPoint in your skills section
- Omitting important work terms or education because you trimmed your resume to fit one page
- Adding a project that you have spent less than a week on .
- You have added job experience that’s irrelevant to the role
- It’s not clear what your current status is. (employed, student, graduation date)
- You added an employer with a name that can’t be found on LinkedIn or Google and you haven’t elaborated
- You have included work experiences or projects where you had a vague or inactive role
- You have no contact details
- You have added links to your profile or projects that are outdated
- Adding  your github but it only has  4 commits, just the academic coursework
- Adding Kaggle, but it only has a single tutorial notebook e.g the Titanic
- Including a language you’ve used for a month and haven’t touched it in years
- Using an email address that is not professional
- Including a detailed description of your graduate thesis that’s not directly related to the role

# How To Make It Stronger

If you have made all those mistakes, here’s a list of things you can do to make your resume stronger. We will divide them into two; great to have and nice to have
**Great to have**
- Ensure your Skills section is at the top of your resume.
- Add working links to your best Github projects
- In the Skills section, also list a few ML data types you have worked with e.g. NLP, Computer vision, time series, etc
**Nice to have**
- If you have had technical or business jobs before, mention how much revenue you increased or how much costs you reduced. Also mention how you did it.
- In case you were promoted before, list your original and promoted positions under the same company heading.
- Your writing should be clear and concise.  Use short and clear sentences.
- Divide your Skills section to show your advanced skills and the skills that are work in progress. This will lower the pressure of having to be an expert in the work-in-progress skills during the interview.

# Resume Summary Dos And Don'ts:

## DON'T:

❌ Use a plain untailored header.
Headers like "Professional Summary" make your resume seem generic. It makes the reader question whether you had time to make to look at the job description.

❌ Write a paragraph.
Long texts on a resume lose your reader's attention.

❌ Write an "objective" statement.
They are outdated. They only take up space that you could have used for more important stuff.
You already applied to the job which confirms your objective.

❌ Use buzzwords.
Don’t list skills or qualities that are hard to assess in an interview. E.g. “Hard-working, detail-oriented” etc.

❌ Only list responsibilities, competencies, or areas of expertise.
Repeating the tasks listed in the job description doesn’t tell your reader whether you’re good at your job or not.

## DO:

✔️ Tailor your header to the specific role you apply to
Examples like "Data Scientist Summary" or "Data Analyst Summary."

✔️ Use bullets
Bullets are MUCH easier to read. Use 1-2 lines for each bullet.

✔️ Use your experience to show how you add value
Use the first bullet to sell your profession and how you help companies

✔️ Focus on specific accomplishments throughout your career.
Share as many case studies of major projects or examples of your greatest achievements throughout your career. Use this as your “greatest hits” or “highlight reel” section to sell your product (you)

✔️ Quantify your results.
Numbers establish credibility. How much? How many? How effective?

It takes 12 seconds to create an impression and it’s the same case with your resume.

Invest in a compelling summary section that will effortlessly draw the reader’s attention and encourage them to read through, that way, your most important qualifications don’t get missed, even if they only read the first third of your document.

A strong summary section makes tailoring your resume easier as it allows you to focus on updating your summary section instead of the whole resume for each position.

# BONUS: 3 reasons your resume was passed over

If you have been struggling with call backs, your resume is probably getting passed over.
Here are 3 reasons why resumes get passed over:
- It sounds like a job description.**
The hiring manager knows the role a data scientist should play, you don’t need to tell them.
- Your resume lacks the ‘scope’ of the role**
  - Scope is the quantitative value that defines the size and complexity of what you own and are responsible for
  - If your resume doesn’t quantify your value, it’s hard to understand the context behind the results you delivered. Read the very important examples listed above.
- Your resume is not easy to read**
  - Your sentences should be clear and concise. Use readable fonts, reasonable spacing,  and clear formatting.
