General Mentorship Expectations/Suggestions
1) Mentorship priorities 💯
Take a minute to think about your mentorship priorities, and explicitly answer two questions:
What skills do you want to build throughout the mentorship?
What would a successful mentorship look like to you both?
2) What do you expect from each other 🤝
You’ll both go into this process with different expectations about it. For the most part, that’s a natural part of learning about the process of mentorship, but there are a couple of things that you’ll want to be on the same page about:
How often will you meet?
When and how will you meet? At least one meeting (ideally by video chat) per week is usually ideal.
What response times can you expect from each other?
Specifically what kind of help can the mentor provide? For example, can the mentee ask the mentor to debug code? Can they get their code reviewed for quality on a regular basis?
3) What your mentorship will involve 🏗
As you kick things off, it’s important to make sure that you’re thinking of the mentorship timeline incompatible ways.
If you’re building a project together, when should the mentee aim to have their data collected? When should their EDA be complete? When should their model be fine-tuned and deployed? Deadlines may evolve, but setting them at the outset of your mentorship is important.
What happens if the project deadlines slip?
Will you leave time for practice interviews after the project is complete?
4) What's next 🚀
If you're doing a project-based mentorship, your first step will be to figure out what you want to build together. There are two factors to consider here: the skills this project will allow the mentee to develop, and the availability of data for that project.
Once you have an idea of the topic you'd like to explore (e.g. house price prediction, marketing strategy, etc), the next step will be for the mentee to find data sources that can be used for the project. APIs and web scraping are the most common data acquisition strategies.
If you can't find good data sources for a project idea, no problem! Pick another idea, and try again. We recommend aiming to complete the data acquisition stage in under 3 weeks or less.
How to communicate effectively during your mentorship
Great mentorships are all about communication. If you and your mentee communicate well, you're much more likely to end up with a successful outcome.
Great communication is all about minimizing ambiguity. Here's a short playbook for how to do that.
1) Set deadlines for work deliverables
Scenario: Mentee pushes some changes to their codebase to GitHub. Four days pass. The mentee really wants to get the mentor's feedback. The mentor doesn't know or is busy with work. Mentee gets impatient. Things get awkward.
Solution: As soon as you start your mentorship, set up a shared GitHub repo. Agree on a review cycle. E.g.: every Monday and Thursday, the mentor reviews the mentee's latest code pushes.
In general, any time you ask the other person to produce a piece of work for you (e.g. a data exploration task, a code review, etc.), including the following as in your request: "What do you think is a reasonable timeline to complete this task?". That way expectations are aligned and you're empowered to follow up without awkwardness if the deadline passes.
2) Set response time conventions
Scenario: Person 1 sends Person 2 a message. Three days go by. Person 1 wants to follow up and prod Person 2 but doesn't want to be a jerk. Things get awkward.
Solution: Mutually agree on a target response time. E.g.: all queries should be answered, or a specific answer timeline provided, within 48 hours.
3) Acknowledge the question spectrum
Scenario: Mentee sends mentor a question that mentor thinks the mentee should really just figure out on their own by Googling. Mentee tried to google the answer but feels they need more guidance. Things get awkward.
Solution: Be as explicit as possible about the kinds of questions that are fair game throughout the mentorship. Be open about your respective needs and make this an ongoing conversation. For example, the following conversation is an example of great communication:
Mentee: "I'm confused about X. Can you explain it to me?"
Mentor: "Actually, X is pretty simple, I'd recommend looking it up on StackOverflow first."
Mentee: "I actually have, and found these links [links]. But I've been working on this for a while and still can't figure out [subset of X]."
4) Life doesn't have a pause button, but we do!
Scenario: Mentee spends the first 5 weeks of an 8-week mentorship collecting and cleaning data, and worries that the project won't be completed in time. The mentee is too shy to raise this concern with the mentor. Things get awkward.
Solution: At the outset of your mentorship, decide on what might happen in a situation like this. Is the mentor willing to extend the mentorship? Might you put the mentorship on pause while the mentee wraps up a tedious (but otherwise not challenging) task like data collection? Note: this may be a good use case for the mentorship pause button!
Another thing to keep in mind: It's better to build out your full-stack (data collection through to deployment) before optimizing each step in the stack. If you're spending >40% of your mentorship on data collection, you may be being too much of a perfectionist about it.
5) Careerealism
Scenario: Mentee has an undergrad in something technical, but 6 months of MOOC experience and not much more. Mentee wants their first job to be in deep learning. Mentor thinks this is unrealistic but doesn't want to squash the mentee's dreams. Things get awkward.
Solution: At the very beginning, make room for a conversation about career objectives. Mentors are by far the best-placed people to help mentees understand what's doable and what's unrealistic. Often the best way to a deeply technical role is through a more attainable one first, but it's always worth having the discussion.

Mentor Mentorship Expectations

At Least 20 hours a week (More is preferred) - If the mentee cannot commit to at least 20 hours per week the weekly meetings will shift to bi-weekly. Remember the time you put in is directly related to how fast you reach your goal.
Mentors' minimum guaranteed hours per week - The mentor will have an agreed minimum guaranteed hours per week (or bi-weekly if the mentee can not commit to 20+ hours weekly) for the mentee. This could include but is not limit to scheduled meetings, meeting preparation, mock interviews, and code review. The Industry standard is 10-to-1 hours, meaning 10 mentee hours to 1 mentor hour.
Come prepared - Come prepared to every meeting, including a quiet area, a camera on (optional but preferred), no distractions, and focus. This includes the documents/resources open. People who know me well, know I put 110% into everything I do, thus I expect you are prepared as well.
Questions and inquiries - All questions/inquiries for meetings are provided 24 hours in advance. If questions/inquiries are related to code on a project the code must be pushed to the remote branch with a message notifying the mentor or pull request in a shared repo via GitHub 24 hours in advance of the meeting start time. The mentee can, of course, ask questions at any time but if the mentor believes they need to prepare a detailed answer then could delay the response.
Open Communication - Communication is key in any relationship and I am very understanding. I want to motivate and keep you accountable thus this requires open communication. Life will happen thus the deadlines and meetings will need to adapt continuously. I am happy to discuss any topic outside of the mentorship scope, my goal is to help you better yourself thus other life constraints can’t always be ignored. PLEASE PLEASE PLEASE, reach out if you are discouraged or lack motivation, I am happy to help and honestly my favourite part about being a mentor. If you don’t want to talk about it, I respect that, but please don’t let several weeks go by without informing me. It can be simple as “I am struggling right now, I need to gather my thoughts and I will get back to you”, but if I don’t know then I can’t help you.
Meeting Days - Meeting days are MWF EDT, with Tuesdays, Thursdays, Saturdays & Sundays if an emergency, is deemed by the mentor.
Reschedule or Cancel  - If you need to reschedule or cancel, give the mentor 24 hours in advance. If at least 24 hours is not provided then that meeting will not be rescheduled for that occurrence. The mentor can decide within reason to oblige to reschedule. If Mentor needs to cancel or reschedule and if they can not meet in the week of their regularly scheduled intervals then an additional week will be added to the mentee’s agreed plan (emergencies happen and that’s understandable but try your best). In the case of a reschedule or cancellation, the mentor will provide the mentee with a link to their calendar, to find a makeup time.
Asking Questions - When asking questions, I expect you to at least attempt to look it up prior to reaching out. (More on this topic in a future document).
Response Time - In the mentorship, direct messaging will be available for questions outside of meetings. With that said we all have busy lives and I am pretty good at answering messages but let’s agree on a 48-hour response before either party sends a reminder.
Modules & Resources - It’s expected that you have read/gone through the week's material that all parties agreed on previously which could include modules or resources prior to our weekly meeting. If worksheets or reading are needed for review prior to a meeting then the mentee needs to have documents saved in the shared google drive 24 hours in advance.
Meeting Re-occurrence - For the job search there will be required resources to complete for various stages, once complete a call will be booked. After completing all required modules, recurring meetings can be scheduled. Meaning I will match your speed, if you complete 4 modules in one week we can have 4 calls in a week (assuming our schedules align). If only focusing on the mentorship project, recurring meetings can be scheduled.
